# Build Personal AI Agents With Privacy, Open Source, And Wide Distribution

## Overview

This teaching distills Mark Zuckerberg's AI manifesto and the design principles behind the Muse personal agent. It covers how to build AI systems that empower individuals through wide distribution, strong privacy guarantees, open source collaboration, and long-term community investment rather than concentrating capability and wealth in a small number of labs. The goal is to create beneficial AI that individuals can trust and direct toward what matters most in their own lives.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When designing personal AI agent systems
- When building AI products that require user trust and privacy
- When establishing AI distribution and open source strategy
- When creating data center infrastructure for AI training
- When training models specifically for agent use cases

## Steps

### Step 1: Define Your AI Distribution Philosophy Around Empowering Individuals

Articulate a clear philosophy that puts technology in people's hands rather than restricting it to a small number of institutions. Identify the core principles guiding your work: empowering people is the source of prosperity, AI's primary purpose is invention rather than automation, and safety comes from checks and balances and balance of power rather than restricting access. Communicate your AI distribution philosophy publicly so users, partners, and regulators understand what your lab stands for and what values guide your decisions.

### Step 2: Build Personal AI Agents That Understand and Act On User Goals

Design agents that accept projects and goals rather than single prompts, then work autonomously 24/7 until those goals are achieved. Give each person a capable personal agent that can coordinate activities, make reservations, manage home school schedules, acquire permits, or coach athletic training. Build proactive suggestion systems so the agent can recommend new projects and improvements based on what it observes, helping users discover valuable use cases they may not have considered on their own.

### Step 3: Implement Confidential Computing So Even the Provider Cannot Access User Data

Build a confidential virtual machine environment that provides cloud convenience with the security of a physically local device. Use technical guarantees—not just policies—to ensure that no one at the company can see the content stored in a user's agent. Recruit security experts with proven track records in end-to-end encryption to architect the confidential virtual machine systems. Publish the architecture details so the commitments can be technically verified by the community.

### Step 4: Design With Least Privilege Access for All Integrations

When users connect their agent to email, messaging, payments, or any other service, start with read-only access and require explicit permission for write or send actions. Never grant broad access by default. Apply the least privilege principle at every step: only add permissions when the user explicitly needs them for a specific task. This protects users who are new to agent products and have not yet built intuition about what access to grant.

### Step 5: Create Fleet Learning Systems Where Agents Share Anonymized Insights

Enable agents across the user base to learn from anonymized insights gathered from the rest of the fleet. Build a tab or interface where users can see ideas and expansions suggested by other users doing similar things. Use network effects to make the product better as more people use it, while keeping individual data private. This turns a single-player agent experience into a collectively improving system without exposing private user information.

### Step 6: Invest in Long-Term Data Center Infrastructure for Local Communities

Build data centers with decades-long commitments to the communities that host them, not short-term speculative operations aimed at flipping sites to the highest bidder. Fund local workforce training programs—such as fiber technician, electrician, and advanced carpentry training—and guarantee jobs to graduates who complete the program. Ensure tax revenue from data centers directly benefits local residents, funding initiatives such as teacher retention bonuses and local public infrastructure. This creates broad-based prosperity and earns community support rather than resistance.

### Step 7: Train Models Specifically for Personal Agent Capabilities

Design models from the ground up for agent use cases rather than post-training a general model for agent tasks. Prioritize capabilities such as discretion—knowing what information to keep private without being explicitly told—relationship-building, and coding. Use training methods that establish clear boundaries and values during the training process: strong guardrails prevent reward hacking and ensure the model learns the intended curriculum rather than finding shortcuts. Build specialized training data for scenarios like making reservations without revealing sensitive personal conditions, where the agent must achieve the user's goal without unnecessary disclosure.

### Step 8: Partner Proactively With Government Rather Than Avoiding Regulation

Work closely with government partners to keep them informed about important training runs and emerging capabilities. Build trusted communication channels across different parts of government so you can address evolving challenges—cybersecurity, bio-risk, economic impact—without relying on rigid frameworks that will quickly become outdated. Take the first step toward establishing industry standards when appropriate, even if it puts your company at a short-term disadvantage, to create legally binding frameworks that bring the whole industry into alignment and prevent any single company from being penalized for doing the right thing.

### Step 9: Communicate Privacy Features Clearly to Build User Trust

Design privacy and security into the product from day one, and communicate those features clearly and repeatedly to users. Do not assume that early adoption means users understand or remember the privacy safeguards built into the product. Revisit and reinforce privacy messaging as the product reaches mainstream scale and new audiences who never saw the original launch communications. Build a brand reputation for taking privacy seriously so users feel confident connecting sensitive services to the agent.

## Examples

### Example 1: Using Muse Agent to Coordinate Family Activities Like Baking Projects and Mountain Climbing Permits

Set up a recurring weekend baking project that is appropriate for a young child and an adult with no baking experience. Ask the agent to use delivery services to source all ingredients and confirm everything is ready before the weekend arrives. After each session, tell the agent how it went so it can update its approach for next time—for example, adjusting difficulty after discovering that cake pops are unexpectedly hard. For an older child interested in mountain climbing, configure the agent to monitor permit availability and automatically secure permits when they open, freeing the parent to simply take the day off and climb together when the agent notifies them.

### Example 2: Using MMA Camera Feedback for Training Improvement

Install cameras in an MMA gym and instruct the agent to watch training sessions and send personalized feedback. The agent can analyze technique, flag moments where the fighter appeared to give up, and identify specific frames worth reviewing. Coaches can use the agent's observations as conversation starters with athletes, surfacing feedback that might otherwise go unsaid. Over time, the agent can suggest improvements to its own feedback pipeline—such as refining the frame selection or adjusting the tone of its comments—making the training experience progressively more useful.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Distribute AI technology widely to create broad-based prosperity
- ✅ Design systems with privacy and encryption from the beginning
- ✅ Invest in long-term community infrastructure for data centers
- ✅ Use open source to maintain competition and transparency
- ✅ Build agents that suggest helpful actions to users
- ❌ Don't restrict AI access to only a small number of institutions
- ❌ Don't hoard AI capabilities in closed systems
- ❌ Don't build speculative data centers without long-term community commitment
- ❌ Don't neglect privacy and security in agent design

## Keep In Mind

- The best advances come from people on the periphery, not incumbents
- Network effects make agents better as more people use them
- Users need to trust the system before connecting sensitive data
- Models designed from ground up for agents outperform post-trained general models
- Discretion is a critical agent capability: the agent must know what is sensitive without being explicitly told every time
- A super intelligent tool benefits everyone most when everyone has access to it, creating natural checks and balances

## Security & Safety Notes

- Implement confidential virtual machines so providers cannot see user content
- Use sentinel agents to monitor for prompt injection and anomalous behavior
- Require human-in-the-loop approval for sensitive actions like payments
- Apply least privilege principle: only grant access when explicitly needed
- Design systems where even the company cannot access encrypted user data
- Establish clear boundaries during training to prevent reward hacking; treat model training with firm guardrails and a well-defined curriculum
- Build secure credential stores so agents access sensitive data only when explicitly needed for a task

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Training models using reward hacking where the agent finds shortcuts instead of solving the intended problem
  **Solution:** Establish clear boundaries and values during training to ensure agents learn the intended curriculum and internalize the right goals rather than exploiting loopholes
- **Problem:** Building AI systems that concentrate power and wealth in a small number of labs
  **Solution:** Distribute technology widely through open source, affordable access, and personal agents to create checks and balances; prioritize broad-based prosperity over concentrating gains
- **Problem:** Data centers built by speculators who don't care about local communities
  **Solution:** Commit to decades-long community investment, create workforce training programs, and ensure tax revenue benefits local residents rather than treating infrastructure as a short-term asset flip
- **Problem:** Users don't know what to do with AI agents
  **Solution:** Build proactive suggestion systems that help users discover valuable use cases for their agents, and design the onboarding experience to demonstrate immediate practical value
