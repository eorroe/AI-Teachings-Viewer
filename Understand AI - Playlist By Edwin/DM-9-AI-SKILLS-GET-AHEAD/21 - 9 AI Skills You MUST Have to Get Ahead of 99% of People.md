# 9 AI Skills You MUST Have to Get Ahead of 99% of People (According to the Speaker)

## Overview

This AI teaching provides nine specific AI skills that separate the top 1% of AI users from the rest, according to the speaker: prompt engineering, taste curation, master prompts, output iteration, system prompts, using AI as a critic, context compression, knowledge base gardening, and personalized learning. Each skill includes concrete, repeatable steps that transform AI from a simple chatbot into what the speaker calls a 'creative operating system' that produces outputs based on your complete role and context. Following these teachings will make you more efficient and effective with AI in personal and professional contexts.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When you want to produce higher-quality AI outputs with fewer iterations
- When you are building AI workflows for yourself or your team
- When you need to produce polished, personalized outputs instead of one-size-fits-all default responses
- When you want to future-proof your AI skills across new platforms and tools
- When you are using AI for business, creative, or learning purposes

## Steps

### Step 1: Master Prompt Engineering

Prompts that include specific context must contain four components in this exact order:

1. **Role**: Tell the AI who to act as (e.g., "act like a marketing expert," "act like a leadership coach," "act like a lawyer"). This tells the AI which body of knowledge to pull from.
2. **Context**: Provide details including your current role, project goals, target audience, constraints, and background information. Include specific data points, dates, names, and numbers so the AI can reference exact facts rather than guessing.
3. **Command**: Give precise instructions about what you need. State the exact deliverable, required constraints, success criteria, and desired outcomes. Do not leave requirements open-ended.
4. **Format**: Specify the exact output structure and delivery method. For example: bullet points for key talking points, a table with columns for risk, probability, and mitigation, a 500-word email draft, or a CSV file with headers name, email, and status.

**Cheat code**: If you have an example of strong output, include it in the prompt so the AI can pattern-match against it. For instance, paste a winning pitch deck slide, a well-written email, or a code snippet with the style you want to replicate.

### Step 2: Build Taste Curation

Taste is the ability to recognize great output and replicate it consistently. Improve your taste through these three actions:

1. **Create a taste library**: Curate examples of strong work in your domain. If you are a startup founder, watch winning pitch videos. If you are a musician, study the best performances. If you are a programmer, review top GitHub projects. Save these examples.
2. **Develop precise communication skills**: Replace vague words with measurable or descriptive terms. For example, say "leader" instead of "boss" if you want collaborative output, or say "max line length of 100 characters" instead of "make it clean," or say "no passive voice" instead of "write better."
3. **Implement universal rules**: Write down repeatable style rules and include them in every prompt. For example: "Write in ninth-grade English," "Use similes over examples," "Keep sentences under 25 words," "Avoid cheesy quotes," "No em-dashes." List five to ten rules so the AI has clear guardrails.

Document your taste explicitly so you can hand it to AI and get consistent results.

### Step 3: Create a Master Prompt

A master prompt is a document that contains everything about you, your role, and your context so every AI interaction draws on your full role and context.

1. **Generate questions automatically**: Tell the AI, "Act as an interviewer and ask me everything needed to build a master prompt for my role." Write down every question it asks.
2. **Answer with maximum detail**: Answer every question. Use the voice-to-text feature to ramble through your answers; the AI will condense them into a clean document.
3. **Generate and save as PDF**: Say, "Generate a master prompt and save it as a PDF." Upload this PDF to every AI platform you use. Because it is a PDF, it will work on future AI platforms that have not been built yet, future-proofing your setup.

### Step 4: Iterate Outputs Ruthlessly

The top 1% of AI users, according to the speaker, iterate to perfection.

1. **Upload your master prompt first**: This primes the AI with your full context before generating anything.
2. **Give specific feedback**: Do not say "make it punchier." Instead say, "Open with an effective reframe that explains how X, Y, and Z connect." Vague feedback produces vague changes. Always point to the exact sentence or paragraph you want adjusted.
3. **Lock good content with Canvas**: Use the Canvas feature (available in ChatGPT) to create a Google Docs-like editing environment inside the chat. Tweak words and sentences manually, then say, "Use that format to write the next 10 emails." This prevents the AI from rewriting everything from scratch each time.

### Step 5: Build System Prompts

A system prompt tells the AI how to behave, whereas a master prompt tells the AI who you are. System prompts are reusable instructions that direct the AI using natural language.

1. **Reverse-engineer from your best output**: After you have iterated to a perfect output, say to the AI, "Write the system prompt that would have generated this exact output." The AI will produce a detailed, structured prompt that recreates that result.
2. **Save as PDF**: Store the system prompt as a PDF so you can upload it to any AI platform, including Claude, Gemini, and Grok.
3. **Convert to a custom GPT for repeat use**: In ChatGPT, copy the system prompt into the Instructions field of a custom GPT. This creates a reusable tool you can share with teammates via link.

### Step 6: Use AI as a Critic

People use AI as a yes-man. The top 1% of AI users, according to the speaker, use AI to expose blind spots and stress-test their thinking.

1. **Activate devil's advocate mode**: In the role section of your prompt, say, "Act as a devil's advocate and stress-test all my assumptions. List every risk and flaw in my plan."
2. **Break down criticism with first principles**: Ask the AI to decompose its critique down to fundamental truths using first principles thinking. This teaches you how to think about the problem, not just what to think.
3. **Capture insights and update your master prompt**: If you agree with the AI's critique, add that insight to your master prompt. Tell the AI, "Update my context as my role so your answers always follow this principle." This makes the AI smarter about you over time.

### Step 7: Compress Large Contexts

You can give AI too much information. When context exceeds what the model can process, output quality degrades. Compress large inputs before using them.

1. **Paste the raw material and summarize aggressively**: Provide transcripts, data, and full documents. Then say, "Summarize this transcript with bullets for key facts, data, and stories." The speaker recommends reducing it down to 10% of his original size.
2. **Identify what was removed**: Ask, "What is missing from the original information? List it out and give me the option to bring it back." This ensures you do not lose critical context during compression.
3. **Lock in the compressed version**: Start a fresh chat window and use only the compressed knowledge as context. This becomes your clean input for all subsequent prompting.

### Step 8: Practice Knowledge Base Gardening

A disorganized AI workspace produces disorganized outputs. Organize your AI knowledge like a curated garden.

1. **Create a project folder for each initiative**: Just name it correctly and make sure the name is very clear. Examples: "Q4 Marketing Campaign - 2026-10-01," "Investment Research - 2026-08-27," "Product Launch - 2026-11-15." If you are on a team, use a shared team account and include the project owner's initials in the folder name.
2. **Upload your master prompt and compressed context into each project folder**: This ensures every project starts with the right role context and background information.
3. **Organize system prompts by department in PDF form**: Keep your best system prompts in clearly labeled folders by department or function. Because they are PDFs, you can reuse them across any AI platform and future-proof your workflow.

### Step 9: Use AI for Personalized Learning

AI is the future of education, but the speaker claims that 99% of people only use it for simple Q&A. Use it to co-create your learning experience.

1. **Write a time-bound learning prompt**: Tell the AI the exact minutes or hours you can dedicate. Example: "Teach me prompt engineering. I have 7 minutes. Break it into 3 key concepts with one example each."
2. **Request conversational language at a specific grade level**: Specify the reading level that matches your goals. Example: "Write a 10-minute research paper on the history of AI for a seventh grader." For complex topics, use higher grade levels to avoid oversimplified metaphors.
3. **Listen instead of read**: Click the audio playback icon and listen to the response while walking, exercising, or doing chores. This turns idle time into learning time.

## Examples

### Example 1: Prompt Engineering for a Business Email

- **Role**: "Act like a professional executive communication coach."
- **Context**: "I am a startup founder reaching out to a potential investor I met at a conference. We discussed AI infrastructure. I want to follow up within 48 hours while the conversation is fresh."
- **Command**: "Write a follow-up email that references our specific conversation about AI infrastructure, asks for a 20-minute call next week, and includes one concrete value metric from my business."
- **Format**: "Bullet points for key talking points, then a full email draft under 150 words."

### Example 2: Taste Curation for Startup Pitches

- **Taste library**: Watch 10 winning startup pitch videos from Y Combinator demo days. Write down three specific patterns you notice in structure, tone, and closing. For structure, note the order of sections. For tone, note whether the speaker uses humor, data, or storytelling. For closing, note whether they end with a metric, a question, or a call to action.
- **Communication refinement**: Replace vague words like "disruptive" with specific claims like "reduces operational cost by 40% for mid-market logistics firms."
- **Universal rules**: Add "Lead with the problem, not the product," "Use one metric in the first 30 seconds," and "Close with a clear ask" to your prompt rules.

### Example 3: AI as a Critic for Investment Decisions

- **Devil's advocate prompt**: "Act as a financial devil's advocate. I am considering rebalancing my portfolio toward AI infrastructure stocks. List every assumption I am making that is wrong, and identify three hidden risks I have not mentioned."
- **First principles breakdown**: "Decompose your critique down to first principles. Which risks are based on fundamental economics versus market sentiment?"
- **Insight capture**: If the AI identifies a valid risk you had not considered, add that principle to your master prompt in a dedicated section titled "Investment Principles" or "Decision Rules" so future financial prompts account for it.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not paste sensitive personal data such as passwords, API keys, or financial account numbers into AI prompts.
- Master prompts contain detailed personal and professional context; store them securely and do not share them publicly.
- When using team AI accounts, ensure project folders and system prompts are access-controlled so sensitive strategy documents are not exposed to unauthorized users.
- PDF system prompts shared via link should be reviewed for sensitive information before distribution.
- AI criticism and devil's advocate outputs are for your internal use; do not forward harsh or unvetted AI critiques to third parties without review.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem**: Accepting the first AI output as good enough.
  **Solution**: Always iterate at least three times, using specific feedback instead of vague requests like "make it better."

- **Problem**: Using vague words like "punchier," "cleaner," or "more professional" in prompts.
  **Solution**: Replace vague adjectives with concrete instructions. Say "Open with a reframe that connects X to Y" instead of "make it punchier."

- **Problem**: Starting every prompt from scratch without context.
  **Solution**: Always upload your master prompt first. This single habit alone can improve output quality.

- **Problem**: Letting AI become a yes-man that only confirms existing beliefs.
  **Solution**: Explicitly ask the AI to act as a devil's advocate and stress-test your assumptions on important decisions.

- **Problem**: Dumping too much raw context into a single prompt and confusing the AI.
  **Solution**: Pre-compress large inputs; the speaker recommends reducing them to 10% of their size, verify what was removed, and use only the compressed version as context.

- **Problem**: Scattering prompts, system prompts, and project files across unstructured conversations.
  **Solution**: Create dedicated project folders, upload role context to each, and organize system prompts by department in PDF form.

- **Problem**: Assuming AI mastery requires coding or technical expertise.
  **Solution**: All nine skills are taught through natural language. If you can write sentences, you can master these workflows.
