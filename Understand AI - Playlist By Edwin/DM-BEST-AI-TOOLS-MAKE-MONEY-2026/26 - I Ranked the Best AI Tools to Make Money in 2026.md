# I Ranked the Best AI Tools to Make Money in 2026

## Overview

Dan Martell ranks 24 AI tools by their ability to generate income in 2026, based on personally testing more than 500 AI tools at Martell Ventures, an AI incubator that launches a new company every month. Each tool is evaluated across three criteria: money-making potential, ease of use, and long-term relevance, then placed into tiers from S (non-negotiable) to F (don't bother), with specific guidance on how to use each tool for business or client services.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When selecting AI tools to learn and invest time in for business income growth
- When evaluating which AI tools to offer as setup or service offerings to clients
- When building or scaling a business and need to prioritize AI automation investments
- When deciding which AI tools to adopt for team productivity and workflow automation
- When you want to understand which AI tools have long-term staying power versus passing trends

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Tier Ranking System

Evaluate any AI tool using three criteria: money-making potential, ease of use, and long-term relevance. Place tools into tiers based on a composite of those three criteria:

- **S Tier**: Tools that essentially every business uses. Add these to your workflow within the first week.
- **A Tier**: Widespread but not universally known. Add these to your workflow within the first month.
- **B Tier**: Niche but valuable. Use if it fits your specific business needs.
- **C Tier**: Can work in rare cases, but most people won't see consistent ROI. Test cautiously.
- **F Tier**: The speaker states it might be flashy, but there is no real value. Avoid entirely.

### Step 2: Prioritize S Tier Tools First

Focus on tools that apply to every business:

1. **ChatGPT** - Use for writing, coding, automation, and learning AI itself. Open ChatGPT, describe the task you want to accomplish, and ask it to walk you through how to use the tool for that task.
2. **Granola.ai** - Install the desktop or browser app to automatically record and summarize meetings across all major video platforms. After installation, it starts working immediately with no additional setup.
3. **Zapier** - Automate business processes across 6,000 apps with no-code workflows. Foundation for modern business automation.
4. **your.com** - Automate outbound qualifying calls, inbound responses, and voice and phone call revenue activities using AI. You need to understand basic prompt engineering to set this up.
5. **Gamma.app** - Generate reports, proposals, pitch decks, and visual outputs. Use to make all business communications more polished.

### Step 3: Adopt A Tier Tools for Specific High-Value Use Cases

Deploy these tools when their specific strengths match your needs:

1. **Claude (Anthropic)** - Use for long-form writing, code review, and analysis tasks. Claude is stronger than ChatGPT at following detailed writing instructions and reviewing large codebases; use it for content-heavy work and switch back to ChatGPT for quick tasks or automation.
2. **11 Labs** - Generate uber realistic AI voiceovers. Use for content creation, marketing voiceovers, and correcting mispronounced words without re-recording.
3. **Gum Loop** - Build visual AI workflows as a more visual alternative to Zapier. Use for financial automation and recruiting processes.
4. **N8N** - Open-source workflow automation for developers. Self-host to avoid license costs and audit code for transparency. Best for complicated workflows.
5. **HeyGen** - Create AI avatar videos from text for training videos, product explainers, and marketing content. High ease of use (8/10). Charge clients to set it up for them.
6. **BuddyPro.ai** - Deploy as an AI brain trained on your company information to handle support emails and internal team questions, powering responses so the team does not need to reply manually to every support email.
7. **Perplexity.ai** - Use as an AI-powered search engine with verified sources to reduce hallucination risk. Positioned after Zapier and Granola in priority.

### Step 4: Use B Tier Tools for Niche Opportunities

Apply these tools when you have specific requirements or client needs:

1. **Runway ML** - AI-powered video generation for marketing collateral, walkthroughs, and video content. The speaker states the learning curve is harder than most tools, but valuable for video creators and marketing agencies.
2. **MidJourney** - Generate images from prompts for brand packages, website designs, and visual assets. Easy to use but niche; comparable tools include DALL-E 3 and Stable Diffusion. Ranked below HeyGen because video is more interesting to Dan Martell than static images.
3. **Leonardo.ai** - Generate concept art and branded visuals. Ideal for designers creating brand material. Note: consistency issues and hallucinations can occur with branded output.
4. **Lovable** - Build apps without coding by prompting the platform. Use ChatGPT to write prompts, insert into Lovable to generate apps. Known for "disposable apps that help run departments." Monetize by selling Lovable-built apps to businesses for $10,000–$15,000.

### Step 5: Avoid or Minimize C and F Tier Tools

Defer adoption of these tools unless your use case directly matches the stated criteria below:

- **Fixer.ai** - AI email categorization and productivity tool. It helps you be more productive in your inbox, according to the speaker, but does not create billable client services or generate direct revenue.
- **Notion AI** - Built-in Notion assistant for writing and notes. Useful if already using Notion, but faces competition from OpenAI's Company Knowledge product. Not a standalone monetizable service.
- **Revio** - AI sales assistant for support emails and social media leads (Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook). Necessary only if Instagram is a primary sales channel for your business; irrelevant for businesses focused on other platforms.
- **Cursor AI** - AI coding assistant integrated development environment (IDE). Super technical and super niche; only relevant for developers.
- **Notebook LM** - Google's AI for analyzing and summarizing uploaded documents, audio files, and video files. Its "Audio Overview" feature generates AI-narrated podcast-style summaries of source material, which is useful for researchers, but it is not essential for most businesses.
- **Gemini** - Google's AI with integration advantages for Google ecosystem customers. Ranked behind Claude for non-Google users.
- **Apple Intelligence** - Branded AI for Apple devices. The speaker states it has consistently underperformed and that few people use it. Avoid until improvements are released.

### Step 6: Implement New Tools Using the Two-Week Habit Stack Method

To actually make money with any AI tool, follow this implementation process:

1. Choose one S or A tier tool from the ranking that aligns with your current business model and income goals.
2. Install it immediately - do not delay.
3. Set it up completely with proper configuration.
4. Use the tool once per day for 14 consecutive days to build the habit. On Day 1, complete one real task with it. By Day 7, use it for two different tasks. By Day 14, it should feel automatic.
5. Habit stack: Connect using the new AI tool to an existing daily routine. For example, open Granola.ai immediately after joining your first video call of the day, or check Gamma.app before every client presentation. Using the tool at the same trigger point for 14 days makes the behavior automatic.
6. Start a concrete project with the tool to reinforce the behavior and measure ROI.

## Examples

### Example 1: HeyGen Client Service Setup

A marketing professional identifies HeyGen as interesting for creating training videos. They install HeyGen, set up their first AI avatar video by inputting text for a product explainer, then pitch the service to local businesses. They charge $2,500 per video, a rate derived by comparing it to the $5,000–$15,000 cost of a traditionally produced training video and pricing at half the market rate to undercut competition while maintaining 70% margin since HeyGen handles all video generation.

### Example 2: Lovable App Development Business

A non-technical entrepreneur learns Lovable to build disposable business apps. They use ChatGPT to write detailed prompts describing client workflow problems, insert those prompts into Lovable, and generate functional apps in hours. They sell these apps to small businesses for $10,000–$15,000 each, automating departments that currently run on spreadsheets. The key is knowing how to prompt effectively and understanding client business processes.

### Example 3: your.com Outbound Qualifying System

A sales-focused business sets up your.com to handle outbound qualifying calls and inbound responses. They configure the AI to ask qualifying questions, route interested leads to human sales reps, and handle routine inquiries. The system operates 24/7, captures lead information, and reduces the time human agents spend on initial qualification. The business owner needs only basic prompt engineering skills to configure the system, and the voice AI interface becomes the primary communication layer for the sales department.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Granola.ai records meetings on your device without informing other participants; use only for personal note-taking, as participants are unaware of the recording
- When selling AI-generated services to clients, be aware of hallucination risk with language models, especially with branded content in tools like Leonardo.ai and MidJourney
- Open-source tools like N8N allow code auditing but require self-hosting and security configuration; secure your servers before deployment
- AI tools trained on your business data can use that data for model training; review data privacy policies before uploading business data to tools like BuddyPro.ai or Notebook LM
- Voice AI tools like your.com and 11 Labs generate realistic audio; disclose AI voice use to comply with communication regulations in customer-facing scenarios

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Installing AI tools but not using them
  **Solution:** Spend 14 days installing, setting up, and forcing yourself to build the new habit. Connect the tool to an existing work habit so it becomes second nature.

- **Problem:** Trying to monetize tools that require skills you do not possess
  **Solution:** Focus on tools aligned with your current expertise. Non-technical users should start with Lovable rather than Cursor AI; designers can monetize Leonardo.ai and MidJourney, but most people cannot.

- **Problem:** Choosing C or F tier tools hoping for income
  **Solution:** Prioritize S and A tier tools. Avoid C and F tier tools unless you have a specific client request or use case.

- **Problem:** Assuming all AI tools will remain relevant long-term
  **Solution:** MidJourney is ranked B tier because you can do a lot of this in other tools. Choose tools with strong ecosystem positions like ChatGPT and Zapier.

- **Problem:** Building workflows in multiple overlapping automation tools
  **Solution:** Start with Zapier as your foundation. Only adopt Gum Loop or N8N if you have requirements Zapier cannot solve.

- **Problem:** Selling AI-generated deliverables without disclosing AI usage
  **Solution:** Be transparent with clients about AI involvement, especially for branded materials. Catch hallucinations and inconsistencies before delivery.
