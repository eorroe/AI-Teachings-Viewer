# This Video Will Make You Dangerously Smart With AI

## Overview

This teaching shows you how to stop using AI for basic tasks (email drafting, summaries) and start using it as a strategic thinking partner. It provides a four-step system: (1) upgrade the information you consume, (2) use AI to red-team your ideas before launching them, (3) map your weekly tasks to identify the 8% of work that requires human judgment, according to the speaker, and (4) automate the remaining 92%, according to the speaker, so you operate as a director rather than a doer.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When you want to move beyond using AI for tasks such as emails, summaries, and building basic slide decks
- When you need to stress-test a high-stakes business decision, product launch, or creative idea before committing resources
- When you want to identify which tasks in your workweek require human judgment (taste, vision, care) versus which can be delegated to AI
- When you feel stuck doing repetitive work (data entry, caption writing, report formatting) that AI tools can handle
- When you want to build a daily 3-minute morning briefing system using AI so you stay ahead of developments in your industry

## Steps

### Step 1: Upgrade Your Inputs

Your output quality is a direct reflection of your input quality. Stop consuming low-signal content and replace it with premium inputs such as frameworks, expert knowledge, and insights.

**Action 1: Reset your social algorithm**
- Open YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok (or whichever platforms you use most)
- Go to Settings → Content preferences on each platform and clear or reset your watch history, liked videos, and explore-page history
- Search for and follow accounts, hashtags, and channels directly related to the topics you want to master (for example: AI news, startup strategy, product management)
- Like, save, and comment on content from those accounts for 5–10 minutes per day so the algorithm retrains within 3–5 days
- On Instagram, open the "Your Algorithm" section in Settings and explicitly select the topics you want to see

**Action 2: Build your own daily AI newsletter**
- Open a tool such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity and use it to scan RSS feeds and news sources in three categories you define (for example: AI developments, startup funding, product management) every morning
- Prompt: "You're my daily AI research assistant. Each morning, find the top three developments in frontier models and AI, robotics, and infrastructure. Summarize each one into two sentences with the source links, tell me why it matters, and format it as a quick briefing I can read in under 3 minutes. Keep it entertaining and easy to digest."
- Read the briefing during a routine 3-minute window (for example: right after waking up, during your morning workout, or while your coffee brews)

**Action 3: Use Notebook LM for accelerated consumption**
- Upload a document, YouTube transcript, PDF, or set of research links to Notebook LM (available at notebooklm.google.com)
- Use the built-in chat to ask specific questions about the material (for example: "What are the three biggest risks in this plan?" or "Summarize the key arguments in one paragraph")
- Generate a podcast-style audio summary, a slide deck, a study guide, flashcards, or a quiz from the uploaded material
- Use the Audio Overview feature on your phone by opening Notebook LM in a mobile browser and tapping the generated audio player
- Practice just-in-time learning: when you need to make a decision, use Notebook LM to pull the exact information you need right now, rather than consuming broad content hoping you will need it later

### Step 2: Red Team Your Ideas With AI

Before you ship your next big idea, use AI to stress-test it from multiple angles. This is a military concept where you intentionally try to kill your own plan before the enemy does.

**Action 1: Find the fatal flaw (pre-mortem)**
- Prompt: "If this project fails in 6 months from now, why did that happen?"
- Let AI work backwards from the disaster to identify the single point of failure
- Use the output to fortify the idea before you launch

**Action 2: Eliminate blind spots**
- Prompt: "You are a cynical, highly successful competitor. Analyze this plan and tell me exactly how you'd exploit the weaknesses to steal my customers."
- Give AI access to your constraints, timelines, and resources for the most thorough analysis

**Action 3: Risk ranking**
- Prompt: "Rank the top three risks by likelihood and impact. Then build a contingency plan for each."
- Turn all your fears into a checklist of vulnerabilities with corresponding defense plans

### Step 3: Map Your Tasks and Identify the 8%

Gather every task you do on a typical week, break them into 15–30 minute chunks, and place them in a quadrant based on whether they are easy or hard for humans, and easy or hard for computers.

**Action 1: List your weekly tasks**
- Open your calendar app (Google Calendar, Outlook, or Apple Calendar) and your project management tool (Notion, Asana, Trello, or a simple text file)
- Review the last seven days of calendar events and the current week's project board
- Write down every distinct task that took you 15–30 minutes or longer, including meetings, emails, content creation, data analysis, and administrative work

**Action 2: Map to the quadrant**
- X-axis: hard for humans to easy for humans
- Y-axis: easy for computers to hard for computers
- Find the top-right quadrant: tasks that are hard for computers but easy for humans
- Circle the tasks that require taste, vision, or care
- These are your 8% of work that requires human judgment, according to the speaker: taste (knowing what looks great), vision (understanding what the future should look like that doesn't exist, that should), and care (emotional connection, the ability to talk to people and show ideas and get involvement and enroll people)

### Step 4: Automate the 92%

Everything that is not in the 8% quadrant should be delegated to AI.

- Identify the tasks in the easy-for-computer, hard-for-human quadrant (examples: writing social captions, formatting reports, analyzing spreadsheet data, scheduling meetings, transcribing recordings)
- Assign those tasks to specific tools: use AI writing assistants (such as Claude or ChatGPT) for content generation, automation platforms (such as Zapier or Make) for workflow triggers, and custom GPTs or Claude Projects for domain-specific repetitive tasks
- Shift your role from executing tasks to writing prompts, reviewing AI output, and making final judgment calls — you are now the director of an AI team, not the person doing the work
- For advanced automation, explore agent frameworks such as Manis AI or OpenClaw that can run multi-step AI workflows end-to-end without human intervention at each step

## Examples

### Example 1: Daily Briefing for a Tech Founder

You are a tech founder focused on AI, robotics, infrastructure, and developer tools. Every morning at 7 AM, your AI research assistant sends you a Slack DM with the top three developments from the previous day in each of your four categories. Each development is summarized in two sentences with source links, and includes a one-line explanation of why it matters to your business. You read it on your treadmill in under 3 minutes and start your day with curated, signal-rich updates instead of junk ideas.

### Example 2: Pre-Mortem for a Product Launch

You are about to launch a new SaaS feature. Before announcing it, you prompt AI: "If this feature launch fails in 6 months and we have to roll it back, why did that happen?" AI returns a list including poor onboarding, pricing mismatch, lack of integrations, and support overload. You address each risk before launch, including building a better onboarding flow and staffing support for the first 30 days. Six months later, the launch succeeds because you anticipated the failure modes.

### Example 3: Task Mapping for a Marketing Manager

A marketing manager lists her weekly tasks: writing social captions, analyzing campaign performance, brainstorming campaign angles, presenting to executives, and managing team morale. She maps them to the quadrant. Writing captions and analyzing data go to the easy-for-computer quadrant. Brainstorming angles, presenting to executives, and managing team morale go to the top-right quadrant: hard for computers, easy for humans. She automates caption writing and data analysis with AI, and focuses her 8% on strategy, vision, and people.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Use AI as a sparring partner, not just a search engine
- ✅ Upgrade your inputs before you worry about upgrading your outputs
- ✅ Red team every decision that seems obvious with three AI prompts before acting
- ✅ Map your weekly tasks to the human-computer quadrant
- ✅ Automate the 92% so you can focus on the 8% that requires your taste, vision, and care
- ✅ Practice just-in-time learning instead of just-in-case learning
- ✅ Ask pre-mortem questions to break emotional attachment to your ideas

## Keep In Mind

- Your brain takes in information and produces ideas based on it: consuming junk ideas produces junk ideas
- The algorithm on any social platform is a mirror of what you engage with, so curate your engagement deliberately
- The speaker notes that AI has no ego, which makes it the ideal red team partner because it will attack your plan without holding back
- The speaker observes that decisions that seem obvious are the ones that need the most scrutiny, because the brain tends to hide assumptions when it reaches conclusions quickly
- The speaker predicts that there will only be two types of people: those who use AI to improve themselves and those who use AI to cut corners, and attributes this distinction as what will separate those who succeed from those who get left behind

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not give AI access to sensitive internal documents, CRM data, or proprietary information unless you are using a trusted, secure tool with proper data governance
- When red teaming business ideas, use non-sensitive versions of plans if you are using a public or shared AI tool
- Verify AI-generated summaries and risk rankings against real-world data before making decisions
- AI can produce hallucinations or biased assessments; always apply human judgment to the output
- If you automate the 92% of your work, ensure you retain oversight and the ability to intervene when AI makes mistakes

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Using AI only for simple tasks like emails, summaries, and slide decks
  **Solution:** Reframe AI as a thinking partner. Use it for pre-mortems, red teaming, and strategic analysis, not just execution.

- **Problem:** Continuing to consume information just in case instead of just in time
  **Solution:** Ask yourself before consuming any content whether you will use it to make a decision today. If not, skip it and use Notebook LM to pull exactly what you need when you need it.

- **Problem:** Falling in love with your own ideas and refusing to challenge them
  **Solution:** Before you finalize any plan, run the three red team prompts: fatal flaw pre-mortem, cynical competitor analysis, and risk ranking with contingency plans.

- **Problem:** Trying to do all the 8% work yourself because you do not trust AI with the 92%
  **Solution:** Audit your weekly tasks, map them to the quadrant, and start with one repetitive task this week that you can hand off to AI completely.

- **Problem:** Identifying as a doer instead of a director
  **Solution:** Physically write down your new identity as a director who orchestrates AI. Every time you catch yourself doing work that AI can do, stop and ask whether you should be directing instead.
