# Give me 99 seconds... you'll master AI prompt engineering and AI-first workflow design

## Overview

This AI teaching provides a practical framework for crafting high-quality AI prompts and building AI-first workflows—workflows designed around AI tools that allow practitioners to accomplish 10 times more work, at 10 times higher quality, in 10 times less time, according to the speaker. It teaches the four essential components of every prompt—Role, Context, Command, and Format—plus how to create a reusable master prompt document (a personal AI configuration file capturing your roles, preferences, situation, and goals) and system prompts (instructions that encode how to reproduce a specific strong output) to consistently reproduce strong results. The core intent is to shift from weak, one-off prompts to structured, repeatable AI interactions that scale productivity while preserving the irreplaceable human advantages of taste, vision, and care.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When writing any AI prompt and want stronger, more consistent outputs
- When building repeatable AI workflows for tasks like client reporting, content creation, code review, or data analysis
- When onboarding to a new AI tool or switching between AI platforms such as Claude, GPT-4 (Generative Pre-trained Transformer 4), and Gemini
- When the user asks how to "get better at AI" or improve prompt quality
- When preparing a personal context document to personalize AI responses

## Steps

### Step 1: Structure Every Prompt with Four Parts

Every prompt must include these four components in this order:

1. **Role** — Tell the AI who to think like (e.g., "You are a senior Python engineer specializing in API design")
2. **Context** — Give the details of your situation, constraints, background, and goals
3. **Command** — Be crystal clear on what you want the AI to do
4. **Format** — Describe exactly how you want the output to look (markdown, JSON, table, or bullet list)

### Step 2: Capture Perfect Outputs as System Prompts

When the AI produces an output you love, immediately ask it to write a system prompt that encodes the specific instructions needed to reproduce that result. Save this system prompt and reuse it for future interactions to maintain consistent quality without re-explaining context every time.

### Step 3: Build Your Master Prompt (Digital ID)

A **master prompt** (called your "Digital ID" in the source material) is a document that acts as your personal AI configuration file. It captures your roles, preferences, current situation, and goals so you do not need to re-explain your background in every conversation. This document should include:

- Your professional roles and areas of expertise (e.g., "Senior Python engineer," "Marketing director for SaaS," "Freelance graphic designer specializing in brand identity")
- Your communication preferences and working style (e.g., "Prefers concise bullet-point responses," "Works best with step-by-step instructions," "Uses American English with Oxford commas")
- Your current situation and constraints (e.g., "Running a solo startup with a 2-person team," "On a $500/month software budget," "Working in a regulated healthcare environment")
- Your goals and priorities (e.g., "Scale content output from 2 to 6 articles per week," "Reduce manual data entry by 80%," "Learn Rust for systems programming")

Ask the AI to interview you with all the questions needed to build this document. Answer each question thoroughly, save the final document as a PDF, and upload or reference it before every new AI interaction to make all responses specific to you.

### Step 4: Stack AI Onto Existing Habits

Identify habits and workflows you already perform regularly (email writing, research, coding, planning, social media posting, meeting notes, budgeting, or client reporting). For each one, practice applying the four-part prompt structure instead of writing off-the-cuff requests. Start with habits you already perform and expand from there.

### Step 5: Ask AI to Teach You

When you want to learn how to use AI for a new task, simply ask the AI: "How do I use you?" or "Teach me how to prompt you." The AI will generate custom tutorials, examples, and practice prompts tailored to your specific needs.

### Step 6: Act as Director, Not Doer

Position yourself as the director and let AI handle execution. It is faster and easier to edit AI-generated work than to create everything from scratch. Focus your energy on taste, vision, and care—the three human capabilities the speaker argues AI will never replicate, and which are where humans hold their biggest competitive advantage over AI, according to the speaker.

**Taste** — the ability to refine work and distinguish quality output from poor output.
**Vision** — the ability to see a future outcome that does not yet exist but should.
**Care** — the intentional attention and judgment applied at the end of a piece of work.

## Examples

### Example 1: Using the Four-Part Prompt for Code Review

- **Role:** "You are a senior security engineer reviewing Python code for web applications"
- **Context:** "I built a Flask API that accepts user uploads and stores them in S3. The app runs behind Cloudflare and users authenticate with JWT tokens"
- **Command:** "Review the attached `upload.py` file for security vulnerabilities, focusing on injection risks, authentication bypasses, and data exposure"
- **Format:** "Return findings as a markdown table with columns: Severity, Location, Issue, Recommended Fix"

### Example 2: Using the Four-Part Prompt for Marketing Copy

- **Role:** "You are a direct-response copywriter specializing in SaaS landing pages"
- **Context:** "I'm launching a project management tool for remote design teams. Competitors use generic language about 'productivity' and 'collaboration.' My differentiator is async-first workflows built for designers who hate endless video calls"
- **Command:** "Write a hero section headline and three supporting subheadings for the landing page"
- **Format:** "Return as a numbered list with each option on its own line, followed by a one-sentence rationale"

### Example 3: Creating a System Prompt to Reuse

After generating an excellent weekly report, ask: "Write a system prompt that captures the exact instructions, formatting, and structure you just used so I can paste it in the future and get the same quality output." Save the result and prepend it to every future weekly report request.

### Example 4: Building a Master Prompt Document

Ask the AI: "Interview me to build a master prompt document. Ask me one question at a time about my roles, preferences, current situation, and goals so you can create a complete profile." Answer each question, then save the compiled document as `master-prompt.pdf` and reference it in all future sessions.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Always include all four parts—Role, Context, Command, Format—in every prompt
- ✅ Write commands as direct imperatives ("Analyze...", "Draft...", "Rewrite...")
- ✅ Be explicit about format requirements rather than hoping the AI guesses correctly
- ✅ Ask for a system prompt immediately after receiving a strong output you want to reuse
- ✅ Update your master prompt document whenever your role, situation, or goals change
- ✅ Stack AI onto habits you already do rather than building entirely new workflows from scratch
- ✅ Ask AI to teach you new prompting patterns instead of guessing
- ✅ Edit AI output rather than trying to write everything from scratch yourself

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not upload sensitive personal data (government IDs, financial records, medical information) into master prompt documents or AI conversations
- Review any AI-generated system prompts for unintended instructions or data leakage before reusing them
- Treat your master prompt document like a personal profile—share it only with platforms you trust
- Be cautious when asking AI to interview you for your master prompt; avoid disclosing passwords, API keys, or confidential business information in chat

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Writing vague commands like "help me with marketing" and getting generic outputs
  **Solution:** Replace vague requests with explicit commands such as "Write three LinkedIn post variations about X for Y audience, each under 150 characters, formatted as a numbered list"

- **Problem:** Re-explaining the same context in every new chat session
  **Solution:** Build a master prompt document and reference it at the start of every session instead of re-typing your background each time

- **Problem:** Getting a great output once but never being able to reproduce it
  **Solution:** Ask the AI to write a system prompt immediately after receiving a strong result, then save and reuse that system prompt

- **Problem:** Trying to create everything from scratch instead of editing AI work
  **Solution:** Generate a first draft with AI, then apply your taste and vision through editing—this is way easier and produces better results

- **Problem:** Forgetting the Format section and receiving output in an unusable structure
  **Solution:** Always specify the exact format you need before receiving the output, rather than asking the AI to reformat after the fact

- **Problem:** Treating AI as a doer rather than a tool directed by human judgment
  **Solution:** Always review, refine, and apply your taste to AI outputs; never publish or execute AI work without human quality control
