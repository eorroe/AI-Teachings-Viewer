# The most powerful AI Agent I've ever used in my life

## Overview

This AI teaching demonstrates how to transition from passive chatting with AI to using agentic AI systems that autonomously execute entire tasks while you direct. The core framework is the "director, not the doer" mindset: define the desired outcome, give the AI clear instructions and examples of expected output, review and refine results, and let the AI figure out how to accomplish the task on its own. Master this approach to achieve higher productivity, as IBM's deployment of AI agents for 270,000 employees generated $4.5 billion in productivity gains, according to the video.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When you are currently using AI as a conversational tool (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) and want to move to autonomous task execution
- When you need to delegate research, content creation, coding, or project building to an AI agent
- When you are a business owner, creative, developer, or knowledge worker looking to scale output without scaling hours
- When you want to automate repetitive weekly tasks
- When you need to direct AI to build complete projects end-to-end (websites, reports, code) without step-by-step prompting

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Three Levels of AI

There are three levels of AI capability, each building on the previous one:

- **Level 1 — Chat:** You talk to AI, give it context, and it replies. Examples: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini.
- **Level 2 — Automation:** AI responses are used in pre-built automated workflows. Examples: Make.com, Zapier, n8n.
- **Level 3 — Agentic AI:** The AI thinks, plans, reasons, and executes entire tasks autonomously. It opens browsers, writes code, creates files, conducts research, and completes projects without your hand-holding. You do not need to master Level 2 before reaching Level 3, because the agentic AI can handle the automation layer for you.

### Step 2: Adopt the Director Mindset (Reverse Prompting)

The biggest mistake people make is treating AI like a smarter search engine or a chat partner. The correct mental model is: **you are the director, not the doer.** Your role is to design, direct, and course-correct, not to execute the work yourself. Reverse prompting means giving the AI the outcome you want rather than step-by-step instructions for how to get there:

1. Start with the desired **outcome** — what is the goal?
2. Let the AI build the **plan** to accomplish the goal.
3. Resist the urge to micro-instruct the AI on how to do the work. The AI knows better than you do at execution level.

Apply the three-part framework for effective AI directing:

- **Have a clear outcome:** Before giving the agent a task, be explicit about what result you want. Ask yourself, "What is the specific output I need?"
- **Give clear instructions:** Provide an example of the desired output, a format, requirements, or a template. The more specific you are, the better the agent performs.
- **Clarify the results:** Treat the AI like an intern. Review its work, give feedback, and instruct it to save that feedback so it remembers for future iterations. As you build your agentic workflow, the AI will become more autonomous over time.

There are AI agent tools available, but do not spread yourself thin learning them all. Pick one based on who you are and go deep on it:

Once you pick your tool, ask it to walk you through its core autonomous features and show you how to run a complete workflow without step-by-step instructions. The more you direct AI, the better you become at it.

### Step 4: Run Your First Agentic Workflow

This step uses a concrete example to demonstrate an end-to-end agentic workflow. Substitute your own goal and tool:

1. Open your chosen agent tool.
2. Write a single prompt that includes:
   - The **research goal** (e.g., "Research the top three digital marketing agencies in Canada, finding their pricing, main features, and what they're doing well").
   - The **output deliverable** (e.g., "Create a simple one-page website that summarizes the findings").
3. Submit the prompt and let the agent execute the full workflow without your intervention.
4. When the agent completes the research and builds the output, iterate by telling it what to change next (e.g., "Now add a section to each one that includes client testimonials").
5. Instruct the agent to share the output with your team or stakeholders:
   - Post the link or summary in Slack on your behalf, so collaborators know the source.
   - Send details via email to the relevant stakeholders.
6. Set the agent to **monitor for feedback**:
   - Tell the agent to check Slack for replies at a schedule you set (e.g., every 15 minutes, every hour, or whatever interval you want).
   - If feedback is received, the agent will either prompt you or, if you instruct it to, automatically apply the feedback to the output.
7. When changes are applied, instruct the agent to notify everyone involved in the thread.

### Step 5: Apply the Pro Tip — Stay in the Tool

Do not copy AI outputs out of the tool and paste them elsewhere (Slack, email, Google Docs). Force yourself to stay inside the agent tool and let the AI handle delivery, updates, and follow-up actions. This trains the AI to understand your workflow and produces better results over time. The more the AI does for you, the more it learns, and the more value you extract.

### Step 6: Repeat and Scale

- Pick **one tool** based on your role.
- Pick one weekly task.
- Have the agent do it for you today.
- As you repeat this process, the mindset shift will click and you will get ahead of the vast majority of people still using AI only for chat.

## Examples

**Prompt to Manas AI:**

**Agent actions performed:**

- Builds a one-page website with the summary, writing the code automatically.

- Emails the research details to Joel Harrison.
- Monitors the Slack thread for replies.
- When feedback comes in, updates the website automatically.
- Applies your follow-up feedback and notifies the team of changes.

Result: Manas AI completed the task in less than 10 minutes—a task that the speaker states would take a human a week or longer, especially without coding knowledge.

### Example 2: Codebase Parallel Work (Developer)

You are a developer working on a complex codebase. You need to fix bugs, add test files, and implement features while also handling customer calls and harder architectural problems.

**Using Claude Code:**
- Open your project in Claude Code.
- Ask it to fix a specific bug, write unit tests for a module, or refactor a function.
- Claude Code reads your codebase, makes changes, and runs in parallel while you work on other tasks.
- Review its changes, give feedback, and let it iterate.

Result: You ship faster because the AI works on the codebase in parallel while you focus on other tasks.

### Example 3: File Management and Browser Automation (Creative)

You are a writer or designer with a messy project folder and multiple browser tabs open for research.

**Using Claude Co-work:**
- Ask Claude Co-work to clean up your project folder by organizing files into logical directories.
- Instruct it to open all research tabs, summarize the content, and create a structured document with key insights.
- Let it complete the project autonomously on your computer.

Result: Your environment is organized and your research is synthesized without you touching the keyboard.

## Best Practices

- ✅ **Be the director, not the doer.** Design the outcome and let the AI build the plan and execute it.
- ✅ **Start with the end in mind.** Define the goal before you start prompting, and do not micromanage the execution steps.
- ✅ **Give examples of desired output.** Show the AI what good looks like — formats, templates, requirements — and the quality of its work will improve dramatically.
- ✅ **Treat the AI like an intern.** Review its work, give feedback, and tell it to remember the feedback for future runs.
- ✅ **Pick one tool and go deep.** Master your chosen agent rather than bouncing between tools and learning none of them well.
- ✅ **Stay in the tool.** Let the AI post to Slack, send emails, update documents, and handle follow-up instead of copying and pasting results manually.
- ✅ **Use the agent to monitor feedback loops.** Have the AI watch for responses, iterate on the output, and notify stakeholders when changes are made.
- ✅ **Delegate a weekly recurring task today.** Pick one task you do every week and hand it off to your agent. This is how the mindset shift locks in.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Agent tools with persistent memory and autonomous execution capabilities—such as Open Claw—can take actions on your computer and on your behalf. Be careful with tools that can make purchases or send messages without explicit permission.
- The speaker observed an example where an agent initiated a purchase on its own. Always review what permissions you grant to agentic tools, restrict access to only the services they need, and monitor their actions regularly.
- When instructing an agent to post to Slack, send emails, or share data with others, make sure you understand who will receive the output and what information is included.
- Do not share sensitive credentials, private keys, or confidential business data in prompts that are processed by third-party agent tools.
- Review agent outputs before distributing them externally, especially if the agent is synthesizing research or writing communications on your behalf.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Using AI as a fancy Google search or a conversational chatbot, then claiming it does not add value.
  **Solution:** Switch to agentic workflows by giving the AI a clear outcome, letting it build a plan, and letting it execute autonomously. Do not dictate step-by-step instructions — let the AI figure out how to get there.

- **Problem:** Trying to learn every new AI agent tool that comes out, and mastering none of them.
  **Solution:** Pick one tool based on your role (Manas AI for business, Claude Co-work for creatives, Claude Code for developers, Open Claw for personal automation) and go deep on it. Ask the tool itself to teach you advanced features.

- **Problem:** Copying AI outputs out of the tool and pasting them into Slack, email, or Google Docs manually, which wastes time and prevents the AI from learning your workflow.
  **Solution:** Stay inside the agent tool. Let the AI handle delivery, updates, and follow-up. The more it does for you, the more it learns how you work.

- **Problem:** Being vague with instructions and expecting high-quality results.
  **Solution:** Be explicit about the outcome, provide examples of desired output, give the AI a format or template, and treat it like an intern — review, give feedback, and have it remember.

- **Problem:** Overwhelmed by the technology and delaying getting started.
  **Solution:** Pick one weekly task and have the agent do it today. You do not need to be technical. The AI is the expert — your job is just to direct it. Action unlocks understanding.
