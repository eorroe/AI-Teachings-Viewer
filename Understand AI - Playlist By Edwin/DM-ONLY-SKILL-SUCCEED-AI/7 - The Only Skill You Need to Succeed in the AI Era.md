# The Only Skill You Need to Succeed in the AI Era

## Overview

This teaching argues that the only defensible trait in the AI era is not technical AI expertise, but the ability to think about AI differently by becoming the director rather than the doer. It outlines five mindset shifts — using AI as a trainer, building taste, developing vision, practicing genuine care, and becoming the director — and provides concrete workflows for delegating execution to AI while retaining human judgment, leadership, and empathy.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When you feel anxious about being replaced by AI and want a framework for staying indispensable
- When you are a founder, leader, or knowledge worker who wants to multiply your output using AI
- When you want to redesign your daily workflows so AI handles execution and you focus on direction
- When you need to build a team culture that embraces AI rather than fears it
- When you want to improve your decision-making, taste, and ability to spot excellence

## Steps

### Step 1: Reframe AI From Crutch to Trainer

Treat AI as a learning partner that strengthens your thinking rather than weakening it. Use AI to understand concepts faster, not to avoid the work of learning.

- Identify one concrete task or subject you are currently avoiding or outsourcing entirely to AI (for example, writing a quarterly report you do not understand, analyzing a dataset you have never worked with, or studying a new topic like microeconomics or prompt engineering).
- Design a prompt or system prompt that teaches that subject using a domain you already know well. For example, if you love soccer and need to learn physics, ask AI to explain every physics concept (force, velocity, trajectory, momentum) using soccer analogies tied to specific players and moments — Ronaldo's free kick technique, a striker's acceleration out of the box, a goalkeeper's reaction time.
- Use the AI session to quiz yourself by asking it to generate practice questions after each explanation, then answer them yourself before checking with AI. Ask follow-up questions to test your understanding before taking any assessment or exam; do not wait until after to test yourself.
- Do not let AI do your homework or write your deliverables for you. Let AI be the tutor; you remain the student. If you cannot explain the output to someone else, you do not own it.

### Step 2: Build Your Taste for Excellence

Taste is your ability to instantly recognize excellence. According to the speaker, "Excellence isn't just random. It's a pattern your brain learns to recognize." Taste grows only through repeated exposure to excellent work.

- Immerse yourself in excellence. The speaker says: "proximity is power. If you want to improve your taste, go get around people creating excellent work." Watch award-winning work in your field such as TED Talks, industry award winners, or masterclass recordings.
- Study the masters in your discipline. The speaker says: "Every discipline has masters." Watch recordings of their best work, such as top-winning pitch competition entries or masterclass recordings. The speaker notes: "I hire the best coaches to teach me things because I believe it's better to pay for the blueprints, pay to be exposed to mastery."
- Use social media as a masterclass, not entertainment. The speaker says: "Follow people that are 10 steps ahead of you. Find the people that are creating content... go use your feed and make sure it feeds you excellence by inspiring you to create great work."
- When you see excellent work, ask yourself: "Why does this work?" The speaker says: "It'll get your brain to analyze things and try to find the patterns... teach your brain to identify the patterns of greatness."

### Step 3: Develop Your Vision Muscle

Vision is the ability to see a future that does not exist yet but that customers or the market will demand. AI gives you the space to think bigger by handling research and scenario planning.

- Block out thinking time. The speaker says: "Every week, you should have a block time called thinking time. And in that time, you literally force yourself to think about the future."
- Use AI as a research co-pilot. The speaker describes asking Grok (using its "Heavy" version) to execute 100 AI agents simultaneously to research robotics market size, unit volumes, pricing, and future projections over 5 to 10 years, then returning with data-backed answers.
- Talk to AI aloud using voice mode while walking, running, or driving. Project specific scenarios. The speaker describes crafting prompts asking for research, history, examples, market size, unit volumes, and pricing projections, then speaking for 5–10 minutes per scenario.
- Ask AI to challenge your thinking with prompts like: "I think I'm thinking too small. List expansion paths I haven't considered, ranked by feasibility and upside, and explain why each one is harder than it appears."
- Study innovations from outside your industry. The speaker cites Henry Ford innovating by applying meat-packing assembly lines to car manufacturing as an example of cross-industry learning. The speaker says: "When you look at the examples like Henry Ford learning the whole assembly system by watching the meat packers in Chicago and he applied it to cars, that's where the innovation came from."

### Step 4: Strengthen Your Capacity to Care

According to the speaker, AI can mimic intelligence, but it cannot mimic genuine care. Caring means demonstrating through actions that you prioritize other people's growth over your own recognition.

- Get to know the people you work with. Ask each of them: "What is your five-year career goal? What personal priority matters most to you right now? What does success look like for you in 12 months?" Record their answers in a private note.
- Align company objectives with each person's stated career goals and personal priorities. During your next one-on-one, share how the person's stated goal connects to a current team objective so they see the link between their growth and the organization's output.
- Ask for feedback from trusted peers or direct reports before giving feedback on their work. Ask: "What is one thing I could do differently that would help you do your best work?" The ultimate form of flattery is asking for someone's opinion; it signals that you care.
- Celebrate wins publicly and give constructive feedback privately. Praise in public using Slack, your team's #wins channel, or all-hands meetings. Criticize in private via direct message or scheduled one-on-one.
- Make other people's needs your own. Document their pain points in their exact words, then build solutions that address those specific issues.

### Step 5: Become the Director, Not the Doer

The winning mindset in the AI era is to direct AI as a team rather than doing the work yourself. Most people treat AI like a faster pair of hands. Winners treat AI like a team they direct. Be the director, not the doer. According to the speaker, "the people who will be displaced are the ones who only ever knew how to do the tasks."

- Apply the director mindset: decide what AI should build and what actually matters — the strategic choices that require human judgment, such as deciding what to build, prioritizing actions, evaluating outcomes, and understanding context. These are the tasks AI cannot perform.
- Switch from push prompting to pull prompting. Push prompting means writing paragraphs telling AI exactly how to do the work step by step. Pull prompting means writing 2–3 sentences describing the problem, your desired outcome, and the audience, then asking AI to ask you clarifying questions it needs before generating the best result.
- Build AI workflows. Map core processes in your life or business — for example, client onboarding, weekly reporting, content creation, or hiring — as raw materials to finished goods. For each process, identify the single step that takes the most time or causes the most errors, then use AI to automate only that step.

## Examples

### Example 1: Homeschooling With AI as a Trainer

You want your child to learn physics and math but they are obsessed with soccer and Cristiano Ronaldo. Write a system prompt that instructs the AI to explain every physics concept (force, velocity, trajectory) and math problem using soccer analogies and Ronaldo-style storytelling. The child studies through their genuine interest rather than forced repetition. The result is faster learning and stronger retention, not weaker thinking.

### Example 2: Using AI to Pressure-Test a Business Vision

You have an idea about entering the robotics market but lack confidence in the research. Open your AI tool and craft a single prompt that requests: (1) a brief history of the robotics industry, (2) current market size in dollars, (3) average pricing for industrial robots, (4) projected unit volumes for the coming years, and (5) a revenue projection model. The speaker describes asking Grok to execute 100 AI agents simultaneously to research robotics market data over 5 to 10 minutes, then returning with data-backed answers that allowed the speaker to enter as an investor with confidence.

### Example 3: Becoming a Director in Finance

Your CFO says they cannot automate financial reporting because they do not know how. Instead of accepting that limitation, direct them to: (1) identify the current reporting workflow, (2) write a prompt that describes the desired output format, data sources, and schedule, and (3) use AI to generate the integration code or automation script. The AI writes the integration code. The human's job becomes designing, reviewing, and directing the workflow, not manually executing it.

### Example 4: Building Taste Through Social Media Masterclasses

You are a chef who scrolls social media daily. Replace some entertainment feed time with content from creators ahead of you in the field. When you see a great dish or technique, pause and ask: "Why does this work?" Study the plating, ingredient pairing, and timing. Your taste improves, which makes your prompts to AI for recipe generation, menu design, and content better.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Use AI as a tutor and research co-pilot, never as a replacement for your own thinking
- ✅ Block weekly thinking time and use AI to expand your vision of what is possible
- ✅ Study excellence and actively reverse-engineer why great work succeeds
- ✅ Get to know your people's dreams and align your organization with their goals
- ✅ Praise publicly, criticize privately, and ask for feedback before giving it
- ✅ Treat every process as a workflow and use AI to automate the bottlenecks
- ✅ Switch from push prompting to pull prompting; let AI ask you clarifying questions
- ❌ Do not use AI to do your homework or write deliverables you are supposed to learn from
- ❌ Do not treat AI as a faster pair of hands while still doing the work yourself
- ❌ Do not consume social media passively; use it as a masterclass or not at all
- ❌ Do not skip caring for your team; AI cannot replace genuine human care

## Keep In Mind

- AI is mastering almost every skill that humans rely on.
- The people who advance are not the most skilled at operating AI tools; they are the ones who reframe AI as a collaborative partner rather than a replacement.
- AI is here to amplify you, not replace you, but only if you learn to use it to add value and reclaim your time.
- According to the speaker, the most enduring companies create value for employees, customers, and communities, not just for founders and shareholders.
- According to the speaker, people forget it is about being a human being, not a human doing. Your clarity, leadership, and care distinguish you.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not input confidential company financials, client data, or personal employee information into AI tools without verifying data-handling policies.
- When automating workflows, maintain human review steps before executing AI-generated code or decisions that affect money, compliance, or people.
- Treat AI-generated content as a draft, not a final output, especially when it will be shared externally or used in high-stakes decisions.
- Keep your most sensitive strategic thinking private; AI tools store or train on your inputs depending on the provider.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You use AI to complete tasks you are meant to learn from yourself, such as homework, first drafts, or research reports.
  **Solution:** Use AI to learn the material faster, then do the assignment yourself. If you cannot explain the output, you do not own it.

- **Problem:** You treat AI like a faster employee and continue micromanaging or redoing the work yourself.
  **Solution:** Adopt the director mindset. Set expectations, review outputs, and refine, but do not take the steering wheel back.

- **Problem:** You prompt AI with the outcome already decided, so the output is only a mirror of your own thinking.
  **Solution:** Switch to pull prompting. Describe the problem and desired outcome, then ask AI to ask you the questions it needs to deliver the best solution.

- **Problem:** You rely on AI for vision and strategy without grounding the output in real-world feedback.
  **Solution:** Use AI to generate scenarios and research, then pressure-test assumptions with trusted humans before acting.

- **Problem:** You automate workflows without mapping them first, so bottlenecks move rather than disappear.
  **Solution:** Write out every workflow from raw material to finished output. Identify the specific bottleneck, then automate only that step with AI.

## Glossary

- **Breakout patterns**: Innovations or breakthroughs from outside your industry that can be applied to your own work. The speaker cites Henry Ford applying meat-packing assembly lines to car manufacturing as an example: "When you look at the examples like Henry Ford learning the whole assembly system by watching the meat packers in Chicago and he applied it to cars, that's where the innovation came from."
- **Director mindset**: Treating AI like a team you direct rather than a faster pair of hands. The speaker describes it as: "Be the director, not the doer." According to the speaker, "the people who will be displaced are the ones who only ever knew how to do the tasks."
- **Excellence**: According to the speaker, "Excellence isn't just random. It's a pattern your brain learns to recognize." It is the result of repeated exposure to excellent work, which builds the ability to spot it instantly. The speaker says: "Your prompts to get AI to do things for you can only improve once your taste improves."
- **Genuine care**: According to the speaker, "Care is people feeling that you want to see them win, not just yourself." It is demonstrated through actions that prioritize other people's growth over your own recognition.
- **Pull prompting**: A prompting approach where you describe the problem, desired outcome, and audience in 2–3 sentences, then ask AI to ask you clarifying questions it needs before generating the best result, rather than telling AI exactly how to do the work step by step.
- **Push prompting**: A prompting approach where you write paragraphs telling AI exactly how to do the work step by step. The speaker contrasts this with pull prompting as the less effective approach.
- **Taste**: According to the speaker, "essentially your ability to spot excellence instantly." It grows through repeated exposure to excellent work and is essential for crafting better AI prompts. The speaker says: "the more you can be exposed to it, the easier it's going to get."
- **Vision muscle**: The ability to see a future that does not exist yet but should. According to the speaker, you build this muscle by blocking weekly thinking time, using AI as a research co-pilot, studying cross-industry innovations, and pressure-testing assumptions with AI.
