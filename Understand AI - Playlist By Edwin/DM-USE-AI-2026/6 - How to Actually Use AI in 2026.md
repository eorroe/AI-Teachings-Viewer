# How to Actually Use AI in 2026

## Overview

This AI teaching provides principles for using AI effectively in business in 2026. It identifies common bad advice that sounds smart but can set you back, and replaces each with actionable alternatives grounded in real business outcomes. The core message is that AI should amplify human capability, not replace it, and that success comes from mastering fundamentals before chasing new tools.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When you are a business owner or team leader considering AI adoption
- When you have heard advice like "fire your team and replace them with AI" or "automate everything immediately"
- When you are choosing AI tools or building an AI strategy for your business
- When you want to avoid common AI implementation mistakes that waste time and money
- When you need to decide how much to rely on AI for customer service, brainstorming, or decision-making
- When you are building a personal or team AI toolkit and want to prioritize effectively

## Steps

### Step 1: Keep Your Team and Amplify Their Capabilities With AI

Do not fire your team to replace them with AI agents. According to the speaker, your team is your advantage because AI cannot build client trust, understand team dynamics, or replicate human intuition developed through real-world experience. Instead, use AI as a co-pilot for your team. Train them, encourage them, and demonstrate AI capabilities on their actual daily tasks so they can complete work faster and with higher quality.

### Step 2: Fix Processes Manually Before Automating

Do not automate a broken process because automation will only make it fail faster. Manually fix and validate your process until you know what actually works. Once you know what actually works, use AI to automate the bottleneck causing the most delays or errors.

### Step 3: Use AI for Simple Customer Service, Keep Humans for the Rest

Do not replace all customer service with AI. Customer conversations are where you learn what is broken, what features to build, and what problems to solve. Use AI for repetitive questions, such as business hours, return policies, or basic account information, but keep humans in the loop for complex conversations. According to the speaker, business is H to H (human to human). The speaker says: "Customer conversations are gold mines... That's where you learn what's broken. You learn what features to build. You learn what problems to solve."

### Step 4: Learn How AI Works

Do not skip learning AI fundamentals. If you do not know how the AI thinks, you will not know its limitations or how to craft effective prompts. According to the speaker, you risk making decisions based on hallucinated data. Learn how AI models process input, generate output, and where they fail, so you know what is possible and what risks to mitigate. Ask AI to teach you AI using simple metaphors and similes.

### Step 5: Start Manual, Then Scale With AI

Do not build everything with AI from day one. Founders and operators do not yet know what they are building or what success looks like. Start manual, document each step and outcome to identify what works, map the exact workflow, and then add AI only to the proven bottlenecks. Do not replace first, then use AI to scale.

### Step 6: Solve Old Problems, Not Chase New AI Trends

Do not build your strategy around the latest AI tools because the landscape changes frequently. Focus on solving persistent problems or problems fundamental to your industry, because these are less likely to be solved by a passing AI trend. Master the skill of your primary platform so that as platforms evolve, you already know how the entire suite works. Think about pain that customers will always have.

### Step 7: Use AI for Insights, Humans for Decisions

Do not let AI make all your business decisions. According to Dan Martell, AI predicts the next word, so by definition it gives the most probabilistic, median, generic answer. As Dan Martell says, the best companies were built on innovation and intuition. Use AI for insights and analysis, then verify with experienced team members or mentors who have successfully made similar decisions. You be the director, let AI do the task.

### Step 8: Use AI to Spark Brainstorming, Not Replace It

Do not replace all brainstorming with AI. AI generates ideas based on patterns in existing data, so it can reproduce common solutions. Use it to spark ideas, but rely on human creativity for breakthrough innovation in your specific industry context. Brainstorming requires creativity and finding nuances from specific industries such as manufacturing, logistics, or healthcare, like Henry Ford learning from meatpackers. Use AI to spark ideas, research concepts, expand on ideas, and test ideas against real customer feedback, but not to drive breakthrough innovation.

### Step 9: Provide Concise, Clean Context

Do not feed AI more context than its context window can handle, or include information that is unrelated to the task. According to the speaker, excess or off-topic context causes "context fraud" — overloading the AI with irrelevant information that degrades its response quality, like "giving somebody 10 maps when they asked for simple directions." The speaker also describes "context rot" — where too much or wrong context erodes the model's ability to identify what is relevant. Include only the data, constraints, and desired output format directly relevant to the task. Remove unrelated information that could confuse the model.

### Step 10: Train Everyone on AI, Not Just IT

Do not leave AI to your IT team. Saying you do not need to learn AI because your job is not AI is like saying you do not want to use a car because you are not a mechanic. AI today is literally programmed using English, so if you can talk, you can use AI. Train everyone on how to use AI to do what they need faster and better.

### Step 11: Start With the Problem, Not the Tool

Do not start by picking the coolest AI tools. Cool technology does not solve problems, and nobody wakes up saying they need AI. They wake up needing to solve a problem. Find a problem that customers already pay to solve or that causes recurring pain, then select the AI tool to address it. Use a framework like the Theory of Constraints (a method for identifying the biggest bottleneck in a workflow) to identify the right problem, then find the AI tool to solve it.

### Step 12: Build a Toolkit of Specialized AI Tools

Do not use only one AI tool for everything. One tool will give you a low-quality, one-size-fits-all solution. Each tool excels at specific tasks such as writing, research, coding, or image generation. Build a toolkit of specialized AI tools and match the right tool to the right problem. For example, use Claude for writing, Gemini for research, and dedicated image generation tools for visuals.

### Step 13: Go Deep on Three to Five Tools

Do not use every AI tool that comes out. According to the speaker, pick "three to five tools that you need for your work that solve 90% of your problems" and go deep on them. The speaker warns that founders and operators get addicted to new tools as a form of "productive procrastination" — they're addicted to the newness, but they actually don't do anything with the tools. Master the tools that matter for your specific role and outputs.

### Step 14: Create Your Own Prompts

Do not just copy and paste other people's prompts. Their outputs are based on their problems, not yours. Generic prompts create generic output. Understand your exact workflow, required inputs, and outputs before writing prompts. Understand what problem you are trying to solve, and then create your own prompt. Use others as inspiration, but customize it for your needs.

### Step 15: Combine AI With Your Unique Advantage

Do not think AI alone gives you a durable differentiator. Just using AI will not get you anywhere because most competitors use it every day without changing their results. According to the speaker, your differentiator comes from combining AI with your proprietary workflows, customer relationships, and domain expertise that competitors cannot replicate. That combination is what creates value that competitors cannot easily copy.

## Examples

### Example 1: Automating a Broken Sales Process

A founder spent months automating a sales process that did not work in the first place. The correct approach is to manually test and fix the sales process until it works, then use AI to automate the proven bottlenecks. This saves time wasted on a broken process.

### Example 2: Customer Service With AI and Humans

A company uses AI to answer simple questions like business hours and frequently asked questions, but routes complex customer issues to human agents. This reduces response time for simple queries while keeping the human touch points that reveal customer pain points and feature opportunities.

### Example 3: Building a Specialized AI Toolkit

A marketing team uses Claude for long-form writing, Gemini for research, and a dedicated image generation tool for visuals. Instead of forcing one tool to do every task, they match each tool to the exact task type for better results.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not input sensitive company data, trade secrets, or customer private information into public AI tools without understanding their data policies.
- Verify AI-generated data and research before making decisions, because AI hallucinates and produces completely made up information.
- Be cautious about automating workflows that handle customer data or financial transactions without human oversight.
- Review AI outputs for accuracy before using them in external communications or business decisions.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Automating a process that does not work. **Solution:** Manually fix and validate the process first, then automate the proven bottlenecks.
- **Problem:** Losing customer insight by automating all customer service. **Solution:** Use AI for simple repetitive questions and keep humans for complex conversations.
- **Problem:** Making bad business decisions based on AI hallucinations. **Solution:** Learn AI fundamentals, verify AI outputs with data and experienced people, and never trust AI with high-stakes problems unchecked.
- **Problem:** Wasting time chasing every new AI tool. **Solution:** Pick three to five tools that solve most of your real problems and master them.
- **Problem:** Generic outputs from copied prompts. **Solution:** Understand your actual workflow, inputs, and desired outputs, then create customized prompts for your unique needs.
- **Problem:** Expecting AI alone to create a durable differentiator. **Solution:** Combine AI with your unique process, relationships, and expertise to build a position that competitors cannot easily replicate.

## Glossary

- **Context fraud**: Overloading the AI with irrelevant information that degrades its response quality. According to the speaker, it is "like giving somebody 10 maps when they asked for simple directions."
- **Context rot**: When too much or wrong context erodes the model's ability to identify what is relevant.
- **H to H (human to human)**: According to the speaker, "Business isn't B2B, business to business. It's H to H, human to human." It emphasizes that business is fundamentally about people and relationships.
- **Theory of Constraints**: A method for identifying the biggest bottleneck in a workflow. The speaker recommends using it to find the right problem to solve with AI before selecting a tool.
