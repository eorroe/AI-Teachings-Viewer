# The 8-Step SCOPE Framework for Writing Better Prompts

## Overview

This AI teaching provides a step-by-step framework called SCOPE — standing for Specificity, Context, Objective, Persona, and Examples — to structure AI prompts so that they produce precise, usable output instead of generic results. The speaker says: "I cut my workflow time from 30 minutes down to just three using this five-part prompting framework. I didn't switch models and I didn't pay extra. I simply changed the structure of how I used to prompt." It also covers prompt chaining, which breaks sequential-step tasks into individual prompts that build on each other for more detailed, actionable outputs that incorporate validated information from prior steps.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When you are writing prompts for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or other large language models
- When AI output consistently comes back generic, vague, or off-target
- When you need the AI to produce work that matches a specific format, audience, or style
- When working on tasks with at least three dependent steps, such as writing a go-to-market strategy or a full business proposal
- When you need to recover a lost lead, pitch a demo, or draft any client-facing communication
- When you want to reduce the time spent editing AI-generated drafts

## Steps

### Step 1: Start with Specificity

Define the exact deliverable before writing the prompt. State the format, length, audience, and purpose explicitly. For example, instead of writing "write a proposal," write "write a two-page partnership proposal for a new distribution deal targeting executive buyers." The more precise you are about length, format, audience, and purpose, the fewer generic assumptions the AI will make. The speaker says: "Most people fail here because they forget that the AI starts with a blank slate. It relies on patterns and if your input is vague, it picks the most average common pattern it can find which is usually generic and unhelpful."

### Step 2: Provide Context

Include the background information the AI needs to produce a relevant response. Answer the questions of who you are talking to, what happened previously, and what constraints exist. For example, instead of writing "write an email about the project delay," write "write an email to our client explaining a two-week delay caused by vendor issues, noting that this is the second delay, so the tone needs to be apologetic but confident in the new timeline." The more context you provide, the less the AI has to invent.

### Step 3: Define the Objective

State what the output needs to accomplish. This is different from specificity. Specificity describes the format; objective describes the outcome. For example, instead of writing "write a social media post about our new feature," write "write a LinkedIn post announcing our new analytics dashboard, with the objective of driving demo requests from marketing directors who are frustrated with scattered data across tools." Be explicit about whether you are persuading, informing, converting, or educating.

### Step 4: Assign a Persona

Tell the AI which role or expert perspective to adopt. This filters the model's vocabulary and expertise toward the right type of analysis or tone. For example, instead of saying "review this deck," write "act as a venture capital partner with 15 years of experience. Review this deck and identify specific gaps in market validation and financial projections." Use a supportive mentor persona for encouragement, or a skeptical investor persona for critical feedback.

### Step 5: Supply Examples

Show the AI what success looks like by including examples of the tone, style, or structure you want it to match. For example, write "write copy for our new launch. Here are three examples of our best-performing ads from last year. Match this exact tone and structure." You can also provide examples of what not to do. Examples are a powerful lever in the SCOPE framework because they turn abstract requests into concrete targets.

### Step 6: Combine All Five Elements for Complete Prompts

When you need a result that matches the specified format, tone, and objective on the first try, combine specificity, context, objective, persona, and examples into a single structured prompt. For example, to recover a lost lead, write: "Write a 150-word email to a software-as-a-service (SaaS) client who visited our pricing page but didn't sign up. The objective is to address cost concerns and pitch a 15-minute demo covering return on investment (ROI). Act as a sales consultant who is empathetic but direct. Use this example of our highest-converting email as a style guide." This gives the AI a complete blueprint and produces a draft that matches the specified format, tone, and objective.

### Step 7: Use Prompt Chaining for Complex Tasks

When a task requires at least three sequential steps where each step depends on validated output from the previous step, break it into a chain of prompts where each output feeds into the next. For a go-to-market strategy, start with: "Act as a market researcher. Analyze the competitive landscape for project management tools targeting remote teams. Identify the top three competitors and their positioning strategies." Then feed that output into the next prompt: "Based on this competitive analysis, act as a product strategist. Identify three gaps in the market that our product can exploit. Focus on features the competitors are missing." Then feed that output into the final prompt: "Using these market gaps, act as a marketing director. Create a three-month launch plan that positions our product against these specific weaknesses and competitor offerings." Each step compounds on validated information from the previous step rather than starting from scratch.

### Step 8: Iterate Using the SCOPE Checklist

If the output is missing required format, tone, or content elements, do not rewrite from scratch. Go back to the SCOPE checklist and identify which element is missing. Add specificity, provide more context, clarify the objective, assign a stronger persona, or attach better examples, then rerun the prompt. Prompting is an iterative process, and bad outputs are often caused by a missing variable rather than a bad model.

## Examples

### Example 1: Writing a Client Email

Bad prompt: "Write an email about the project delay."

Good prompt using SCOPE: "Write an email to our client explaining a two-week delay caused by vendor issues. Note that this is the second delay, so the tone needs to be apologetic but confident in the new timeline."

Bad prompt: "Write a social media post about our new feature."

Good prompt using SCOPE: "Write a LinkedIn post announcing our new analytics dashboard. The objective is to drive demo requests from marketing directors who are frustrated with scattered data across tools."

Bad prompt: "Critique this pitch deck."

Third prompt (fed output from the second): "Using these market gaps, act as a marketing director. Create a three-month launch plan that positions our product against these specific weaknesses and competitor offerings."

## Best Practices

- ✅ Always define length, format, audience, and purpose in every prompt
- ✅ Provide as much relevant context as possible so the AI does not have to guess
- ✅ State the objective explicitly by describing what success looks like
- ✅ Assign a specific persona whenever you need expert-level analysis or a defined tone
- ✅ Include examples of the style, structure, or tone you want the output to match
- ✅ Structure prompts visually with line breaks and bullet points rather than dense paragraphs
- ✅ Use prompt chaining for complex tasks instead of cramming everything into one request
- ✅ According to the speaker, focus on specificity and context first — those two elements alone can resolve 80% of bad outputs
- ✅ Iterate by returning to the SCOPE checklist rather than rewriting from scratch

## Keep In Mind

- You do not need all five SCOPE elements for every prompt — use as many of the five elements as the task requires
- According to the speaker, specificity is non-negotiable and is the foundation of every well-structured prompt
- Context is required whenever the AI is blind to the history or the relationship behind the task
- Objective matters most when the output needs to persuade, convince, convert, or educate
- Persona is most useful when you need specialized knowledge or a specific critical perspective
- Examples are most powerful when you need to match a specific brand voice or style
- If you are in a rush, focus on specificity and context only — they deliver the greatest accuracy and relevance gains for the effort invested

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not include confidential client data, passwords, API keys, or sensitive business information in prompts, especially when using AI tools hosted by external providers
- Verify AI-generated outputs before sending them to clients, vendors, investors, regulators, or other third parties
- Be aware that AI tools retain or use your prompt data depending on the provider's stated data handling and training policies
- If you attach examples containing real client communications, redact any personally identifiable information or proprietary details

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** The AI produces a generic, average result.
  **Solution:** Add specificity by stating the exact format, length, audience, and purpose of the deliverable.

- **Problem:** The AI invents details that are wrong or missing from the real situation.
  **Solution:** Provide explicit context covering who you are talking to, what happened previously, and what constraints exist.

- **Problem:** The output does not achieve the intended goal or call to action.
  **Solution:** Define the objective clearly by stating what the output needs to accomplish — whether that is persuading, converting, informing, or educating.

- **Problem:** The feedback or writing style is too generic and lacks expert depth.
  **Solution:** Assign a specific persona, such as "venture capital partner with 15 years of experience," to force the model to access the right vocabulary and expertise.

- **Problem:** The AI guesses the wrong tone, style, or structure.
  **Solution:** Provide concrete examples of the tone, structure, or style you want the output to match.

- **Problem:** The prompt is a dense block of text that the AI partially misinterprets.
  **Solution:** Structure the prompt visually with line breaks and bullet points so that each element — context, objective, persona, examples — is clearly separated and easy to scan.

## Glossary

- **Generic result**: An output that relies on the model's most common pattern because the prompt lacked specificity, context, or examples. The speaker describes generic results as what happens when "your input is vague, it picks the most average common pattern it can find which is usually generic and unhelpful."
- **Precise output**: An output that matches the specified format, tone, and objective because the prompt included specificity (length, format, audience, purpose), context (background, constraints, relationships), objective (what the output needs to accomplish), persona (the role or expertise to adopt), and examples (what success looks like).
- **SCOPE framework**: An acronym for Specificity, Context, Objective, Persona, and Examples — five elements that turn vague requests into precise instructions.
