# 99% of People Use AI Wrong — Here's Why

## Overview

According to the speaker, this AI teaching relays the claim that 99% of people are unaware of the 2026 shift toward AI agents. The speaker observes that treating AI like a traditional search engine—asking one question, getting one answer, and stopping—causes poor AI outputs. The speaker identifies three critical mistakes: using AI for single-turn lookups instead of conversations, focusing on chatbots rather than AI agents (systems that plan, execute, and run workflows autonomously), and copying prompts without understanding the underlying principles. According to the speaker, 2026 is the pivotal year for AI agents, and mastering AI fundamentals—studying the principles behind effective prompts, constructing your own prompts, and building prompt engineering skill (designing effective inputs for AI systems) rather than relying on borrowed templates—is the skill that matters most in 2026.

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Treating AI Like a Search Engine

Do not ask one question, receive one answer, and end the session. According to the speaker, modern AI tools like ChatGPT and Claude are designed for multi-turn conversation (extended back-and-forth dialogue), not quick lookups. When you ask once and leave, you are not using AI to its full potential. Engage in back-and-forth dialogue, provide context, refine your requests, and iterate on the output to get more actionable results. The speaker defines poor AI outputs as the result of single-turn lookups—asking one question, getting one answer, and stopping—rather than engaging in multi-turn conversation.

### Step 2: Shift Focus From Chatbots to AI Agents

According to the speaker, AI agents are distinct from chatbots. AI agents do not just answer questions—they take action, plan sequences of tasks, execute them, and run entire workflows autonomously. According to the speaker, by the end of 2026, 40% of enterprise applications will include AI agents, up from less than 5% right now. Learn what AI agents are and how they operate instead of settling for basic conversational tools.

### Step 3: Learn Prompt Fundamentals, Do Not Just Copy Prompts

Avoid copying prompts from the internet without understanding why they work. The speaker compares this to copying someone else's gym routine without understanding muscle groups: you may see short-term results, but you will not achieve optimal outcomes. Optimal outcomes, according to the speaker, come from studying the principles behind effective prompts, learning how to construct your own, and building the skill of prompt engineering (designing effective inputs for AI systems) rather than relying on borrowed templates.

### Step 4: Build Systems, Not Just Use Them

According to the speaker, people generating income with AI in 2026 understand the fundamentals and know how to build systems. Move beyond using AI as a tool for individual tasks and start designing workflows that connect tools—such as Google Sheets, Notion, CRMs, and email—into cohesive systems (integrated workflows where connected tools work together). Focus on creating repeatable, scalable AI-driven processes.

### Step 5: Automate Workflows That Run Continuously

Design AI workflows that execute tasks without your continuous input. Set up systems where AI handles planning, content creation, execution, and reporting on your behalf. For example, build a workflow that monitors your CRM for new leads, drafts personalized outreach emails, schedules follow-up tasks in your project management tool, and generates a weekly report on conversion rates. According to the speaker, this turns AI from a productivity helper into a system that operates continuously, automating workflows that run even while you sleep.

### Example 1: Conversation vs. Single Query

Instead of asking AI "What is a marketing strategy?" and accepting the first response, treat it like a consultation. Provide context about your business, ask follow-up questions, request revisions, have AI analyze your specific market, and iterate until you have a strategy tailored to your business. This conversational approach yields more actionable results than a single search-style query.

### Example 2: Building an AI Agent Workflow

Create an AI agent that monitors your CRM for new leads, drafts personalized outreach emails, schedules follow-up tasks in your project management tool, and generates a weekly report on conversion rates—all without manual intervention. This is the difference between using a chatbot to write one email and deploying an AI agent that runs your entire lead management system.

Build a workflow where AI pulls deal data from your Google Sheets sales tracker, updates your Notion database, sends a summary to your team via Slack, and automatically creates follow-up action items in your project management tool.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Verify all AI-generated outputs before acting on them, especially when AI is connected to tools like Gmail, Salesforce, QuickBooks, or other systems that send emails, update customer records, or modify financial data
- Be cautious when automating workflows that interact with PII (personally identifiable information), financial records, healthcare data, or external services
- Review AI-generated content for accuracy, factual correctness, bias, and unintended consequences before deploying it in professional settings
- Use OAuth 2.0, API keys with least-privilege scopes, and role-based access controls when connecting AI agents to third-party tools and databases
