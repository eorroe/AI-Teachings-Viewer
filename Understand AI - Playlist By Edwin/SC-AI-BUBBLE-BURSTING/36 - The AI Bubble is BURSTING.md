# The AI Bubble is BURSTING

## Overview

According to the speaker, this AI teaching examines why AI-driven developer displacement predictions—specifically the claim that AI would replace every developer overnight—have failed to materialize despite corporate investment in AI. According to the speaker, companies poured significant amounts into AI without achieving the promised returns. According to the speaker, AI hiring frenzies peaked one year ago and have since died down, with hiring slowing. According to the speaker, developers with strong fundamentals in computer science and software engineering will remain essential even as AI tools mature.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When evaluating AI tools for your development workflow or company adoption
- When considering AI-related career moves or job market expectations
- When deciding how deeply to rely on AI-generated code in production
- When assessing vendor promises about AI capabilities and ROI
- When mentoring junior developers who fear AI will replace them

## Steps

### Step 1: Assess AI tool output with skepticism

Treat AI-generated code and content as a first draft, not a final product. Review every output for accuracy, especially edge cases and the final 10% of functionality—error handling, input validation, and integration with existing systems—that differentiates a working app from a broken one.

### Step 2: Use AI as a productivity accelerator, not a replacement

Integrate AI into your workflow to accelerate code generation, API design exploration, and repetitive implementation tasks. Keep ownership of system architecture, debugging, and final delivery in human hands.

### Step 3: Strengthen fundamentals instead of chasing hype

Invest time in data structures, algorithms, system design, and debugging skills. These remain the differentiators when AI tools fail or produce brittle results.

### Step 4: Evaluate AI vendor claims against real-world stability

Before committing to an AI platform or API, review the vendor's public changelog and API versioning policy for breaking changes and feature removals over the past 12 months. Treat sudden removals or unannounced breaking changes—such as the speaker's example of OpenAI pushing GPT5 and breaking custom GPT—as a concrete risk to your product.

### Step 5: Separate market cycles from long-term trends

Recognize that AI, like web3 and NFTs, is subject to hype cycles. Hiring frenzies and inflated expectations will subside; sustained value comes from using AI as a practical tool rather than expecting it to replace developers overnight.

## Examples

### Example 1: Evaluating AI-generated code for production

A developer uses AI to scaffold a feature. The AI completes 90% of the implementation, but the remaining 10% involves error handling, input validation, and integration with existing systems. The developer reviews, tests, and fixes the AI output before merging, ensuring the app works reliably.

### Example 2: Responding to AI-driven layoff fears

A junior developer hears that AI will replace entry-level coding roles. Instead of abandoning the field, they focus on learning debugging, testing, and system design, recognizing that AI handles routine tasks while humans resolve ambiguity and edge cases.

### Example 3: Assessing an AI vendor before adoption

A company considers adopting an AI chatbot platform. Before signing, they verify whether the vendor has a history of removing features or breaking existing integrations, protecting themselves from sudden migration costs.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Review all AI-generated code before merging; never assume it is correct
- ✅ Use AI to accelerate code generation and repetitive implementation tasks while maintaining ownership of design decisions
- ✅ Strengthen debugging and fundamentals skills so you can handle the last 10% AI misses
- ✅ Track vendor stability and API continuity before committing to an AI platform
- ✅ Treat AI hiring hype as cyclical; prioritize proven skills over buzzword compliance

## Keep In Mind

- AI-generated code requires human review for error handling, input validation, and integration with existing systems.
- AI hiring frenzies in 2023-2024 proved temporary and tied to hype cycles, not permanent market shifts in developer demand, according to the speaker.
- Vendor instability, including unannounced breaking API changes and sudden feature removals—such as the speaker's example of OpenAI pushing GPT5 and breaking custom GPT—creates concrete migration and maintenance costs
- The developers who thrive will be those with strong fundamentals, not just prompt engineering skills

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never trust AI-generated code with sensitive operations, authentication flows, or data handling without manual review
- Audit AI outputs for hallucinated logic, incorrect API usage, and security vulnerabilities before deployment
- Maintain human oversight on any AI system that processes user data or makes business-critical decisions

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing AI hype and assuming it will replace developers overnight
  **Solution:** Focus on building fundamental skills and use AI as a tool, not a career threat

- **Problem:** Deploying AI-generated code without testing or review
  **Solution:** Treat AI output as a draft; review, test, and validate all code before merging

- **Problem:** Over-investing in AI tools based on vendor marketing without proof of ROI
  **Solution:** Pilot AI solutions on small, measurable tasks before scaling adoption

- **Problem:** Panicking about job security due to AI-driven hiring freezes
  **Solution:** Understand that hiring cycles fluctuate; sustained employability comes from core engineering ability

- **Problem:** Building deep integrations with AI platforms that have unstable APIs
  **Solution:** Monitor vendor change logs and build abstraction layers to reduce migration risk
