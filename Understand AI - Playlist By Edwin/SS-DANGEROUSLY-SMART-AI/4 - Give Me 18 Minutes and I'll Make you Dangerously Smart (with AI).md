# Give Me 18 Minutes and I'll Make you Dangerously Smart (with AI)

## Glossary

- **AIM protocol:** A prompting structure where you Act in a role, provide specific Input, and define the Mission.
- **DRAG framework:** A four-category task-delegation system: D=Rafting, R=Research, A=Analysis, G=Grunt Work.
- **Intelligent Fool:** The practice of asking AI the most basic questions about concepts you should understand, requesting repeated simplifications, and embracing the feeling of being wrong to overcome ego and build neuroplasticity.
- **Intelligent Gym:** A learning approach where you study a concept first for 30 minutes, then use AI to quiz you at four progressively harder levels—Level 1: high school student, Level 2: college student, Level 3: executive job interview, Level 4: irate boss—to build deep, durable understanding rather than surface familiarity. These four levels are the speaker's named progressive overload system.
- **Zone 1:** Tasks with capped payoffs that flatten into diminishing returns and should be delegated to AI. Specific criteria: value flatlines after a point and additional effort produces no upside. Examples include formatting slides, writing internal emails, filling out expense reports, attending For Your Information (FYI) meetings, choosing fonts, or designing internal slides.
- **Zone 2:** Tasks with uncapped payoffs that require human intuition, taste, decision-making, or deep relationship building and must be retained by the human.
- **FYI meetings:** Meetings that, per a Harvard Business Review study cited in the video, cause CEOs to waste 72% of their time because they produce no meaningful outcome or decision.
- **Intelligent Hill:** The four-level prompting sophistication ladder: one-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought, and agentic prompting.

## Overview

According to the speaker, this AI teaching provides a four-step framework to use AI as a tool for building durable career-long cognitive capacity rather than just daily task throughput. According to the speaker, the framework teaches you how to categorize tasks into Zone 1 (delegate to AI) and Zone 2 (retain for human judgment), how to climb the Intelligent Hill (one-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought, agentic), how to use AI as a training tool for deep conceptual mastery, and how to adopt a beginner's mindset to accelerate expertise. According to the speaker, the goal is to stop treating AI like a calculator and start using it to train your brain, building durable intelligence.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When you want to stop using AI only for quick answers and start using it to train your brain
- When you need to decide which tasks to delegate to AI versus which ones require your own judgment
- When you want to improve your prompting skills beyond basic zero-shot requests
- When you are learning a new concept and want AI to act as a quiz master rather than a summarizer
- When you feel embarrassed asking basic questions and want to overcome ego-driven knowledge gaps
- When you want to use AI to build mastery through resistance instead of outsourcing your thinking

## Steps

### Step 1: Intelligent Laziness — Outsource Zone 1 Tasks Using the DRAG Framework

Use the DRAG framework to delegate tasks with capped payoffs—work where the value flatlines after a point and additional effort produces no upside, such as formatting slides, writing internal emails, filling out expense reports, attending FYI meetings, choosing fonts, or designing internal slides—to AI, freeing you to focus on high-value, uncapped-payoff work.

- **D = Drafting:** Give AI the AIM protocol — Act in a role, use specific Input, define the Mission — to overcome the blank page problem. Use AI to generate first drafts of emails, code, or presentations. According to the speaker, the first draft will be imperfect; its job is to unblock your brain, not to be final.
- **R = Research:** Use deep research features in ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude to gather competitive intel, summarize large documents, and extract patterns. AI fires search queries, cross-checks sources, and returns consolidated results in 10 minutes instead of the week-long research project it would take a human consultant.
- **A = Analysis:** Let AI take the first pass at analyzing unstructured data — spreadsheets, open-ended survey responses, interview transcripts, or long-form reports — to identify patterns, anomalies, and connections humans overlook. According to the speaker, use this when speed of pattern recognition matters more than your first impression, but always validate findings against your own judgment before acting.
- **G = Grunt Work:** Delegate formatting, translating, tabulating, and data cleaning to AI. These tasks have capped payoffs and should not consume your creative energy. Examples include reformatting a 100-row spreadsheet, converting notes into a structured table, or cleaning up inconsistent date and name formats across a dataset.

**Decision rule:** Only apply DRAG to Zone 1 tasks — work that reaches a zone of diminishing returns. Do not outsource Zone 2 tasks that require human intuition, taste, decision-making, or deep relationship building.

### Step 2: Intelligent Hill — Climb the Four Prompting Camps

Do not treat AI like a calculator that gives the same answer every time. The same prompt can produce a different output each time because the model samples from a probability distribution over possible next tokens. Stop using zero-shot prompts and deliberately climb the following camps:

- **Camp 1 — One-Shot Prompting:** Provide one clear example alongside your request so the model does not guess blindly. Example: ask AI to write a LinkedIn post in the style of a specific post you paste into the prompt.
- **Camp 2 — Few-Shot Prompting:** Provide examples so the model can detect your desired style, tone, and substance. Attach documents, links, or prior work to ground the model and reduce hallucination.
  - Pro tip: ask AI to explain the pattern it detected back to you before generating new content. This forces the model to articulate its reasoning and trains you to recognize patterns in your own thinking.
- **Camp 3 — Chain of Thought Reasoning:** Ask AI to show its work before giving you a final answer. Use phrases like "Think step-by-step," "Tell me why you think so," and "Show me your thinking for each step." This reduces hallucinations and gives you visibility into the reasoning process.
- **Camp 4 — Agents:** Combine roles into a single agentic prompt. Example: "Do deep research on trends on a specific topic, analyze and cross-reference all the trends to find the three most important ones, and draft a one-page memo summarizing the findings."

**Action:** According to the speaker, take any prompt you are about to use and try to move it to the next camp on the hill.

### Step 3: Intelligent Gym — Challenge Yourself for Transformation Tasks

The speaker warns that relying on AI to do your thinking for you causes mental atrophy. Instead, use AI differently depending on the type of task:

- **Information tasks:** Let AI handle the work — summarize content, generate first drafts, and conduct research.
- **Transformation tasks:** Let AI challenge your understanding — quiz you, ask difficult questions, and stress-test your knowledge.
- **Four-level progressive quiz system:**
  1. Study a concept yourself first for 30 minutes.
  2. Paste the concept text into AI and prompt: "I need to master this concept. Quiz me on it."
  3. Ask AI to apply progressive overload at four levels:
     - Level 1: Quiz you like a high school student.
     - Level 2: Ask questions like a college student.
     - Level 3: Grill you like an executive job interview.
     - Level 4: Challenge you like an irate boss who thinks you are unprepared.

According to the speaker, this builds deep, durable understanding instead of surface familiarity.

### Step 4: Intelligent Fool — Adopt the Beginner's Mind

The video speaker identifies ego as the biggest obstacle to intelligence. Switch from a "know-it-all" culture to a "learn-it-all" culture.

- Pick a named concept in your field that comes up in meetings or conversations but that you cannot clearly explain to someone else — for example, "attribution modeling" in marketing, "networking foundation" in game dev, or "Burst Compiler" in Unity.
- Ask AI the most basic questions you can think of about that topic.
- Ask AI to explain the concept like you are 10 years old.
- Ask AI to simplify the explanation three more times in a row.
- Accept that feeling ridiculous is part of the process. According to the speaker, you are building neuroplasticity when you are making errors, and that discomfort indicates you are learning.

## Examples

### Example 1: Using DRAG to Free Up Time for Strategy

You have 10 internal FYI meetings and 3 customer strategy sessions this week. Apply the DRAG framework:

- **Drafting:** Use AI with the AIM protocol to draft talking points for the three strategy sessions.
- **Research:** Use AI deep research to summarize competitive moves affecting your strategy.
- **Analysis:** Ask AI to analyze customer feedback and extract the top three patterns.
- **Grunt work:** Use AI to clean and tabulate data from the FYI meetings.

Use the reclaimed time to obsess over the strategy sessions themselves, which are Zone 2 tasks.

### Example 2: Climbing the Hill with a Presentation Request

Instead of asking "Write a presentation on AI trends," climb the camps:

- **One-shot:** Paste one previous presentation and ask AI to match its style.
- **Few-shot:** Paste five previous presentations, ask AI to explain the tone pattern back to you, then generate a new deck.
- **Chain of thought:** Attach a rough draft and ask AI to list the top three areas of improvement, explain why each matters, and suggest fixes step-by-step.
- **Agent:** Ask AI to research trends, cross-reference them, and draft a one-page memo summarizing the three most important findings.

### Example 3: Using the Intelligent Gym to Master a Concept

You need to master "network effects" for an upcoming product review.

1. Study the concept yourself for 30 minutes.
2. Open AI and prompt: "I need to master network effects. Quiz me on it."
3. Ask for progressive overload:
   - "Quiz me like I am a high school student."
   - "Ask me questions like I am a college student."
   - "Grill me like you're interviewing me for an executive product role."
   - "Challenge me like an irate boss who thinks I'm unprepared."

### Example 4: The Intelligent Fool on a Knowledge Gap

You are a marketing director but never fully understood attribution modeling.

1. Open AI and ask: "What is attribution modeling?"
2. Ask: "Explain it to me like I am 10 years old."
3. Ask AI to simplify the explanation three more times in a row.
4. Ask AI to quiz you on it using the progressive overload ladder.

## Best Practices

- Apply the DRAG framework only to Zone 1 tasks with capped payoffs
- Reserve Zone 2 tasks — customer interactions, product design, pricing, relationships — for yourself
- Move every prompt up at least one camp on the Intelligent Hill
- Ask AI to explain detected patterns back to you before generating new content
- Use chain of thought prompts with "Think step-by-step" and "Show me your thinking"
- Use AI to support transformation tasks without replacing your own effort
- Ask AI to quiz you at progressively harder levels when mastering a concept
- Practice the Intelligent Fool by asking simple questions about topics everyone else thinks you know
- Ask AI to explain concepts like you are 10 years old, and repeat the simplification three more times in a row

## Keep In Mind

- The same prompt can produce varying outputs each time because the model samples from a probability distribution over possible next tokens. Always review and verify output rather than treating any single response as definitive.
- According to the speaker, 70% to 80% of their repetitive tasks tend to be in Zone 1: drafting emails, summarizing meetings, formatting documents, transcribing notes, and cleaning data. All of these are DRAG candidates.
- According to the speaker, resistance and discomfort build long-term intelligence, not through convenience.
- According to the speaker, neuroplasticity happens when you are making errors and feeling frustration.
- According to the speaker, discomfort indicates you are learning.
- According to the speaker, top performers deliberately slow down to build long-term intelligence, even after becoming fast and efficient with AI.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not paste confidential business strategy, trade secrets, or sensitive customer data into public AI models.
- Verify AI-generated research by asking the model to check its own work and identify what is missing.
- Treat AI outputs as first drafts, not as authoritative final answers. Review and edit before acting on them.
- Be aware that AI can hallucinate facts, statistics, and citations. Cross-check claims that carry real-world consequences.
- Use AI to accelerate learning, but retain ownership of decisions that involve human judgment, ethics, or stakeholder impact.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You treat all tasks as equally important and outsource everything to AI.
  **Solution:** Use the Zone 1 versus Zone 2 framework. Only outsource capped-payoff tasks. Keep uncapped-payoff tasks that require taste, intuition, and human connection for yourself.

- **Problem:** You rely on zero-shot prompting and blame AI when the output is poor.
  **Solution:** Climb the Intelligent Hill. Add examples, ground the model with context, ask for chain of thought reasoning, or switch to an agentic prompt.

- **Problem:** You use AI to write your LinkedIn posts, fix your resume, and summarize books for you.
  **Solution:** That is like going to the gym and asking someone else to lift weights for you. For transformation tasks, use AI as a tool that challenges and quizzes you, not as a replacement for your own thinking.

- **Problem:** You avoid asking AI basic questions because you fear looking incompetent.
  **Solution:** Adopt the Intelligent Fool mindset. AI does not judge. Ask simple questions, request repeated simplifications, and treat discomfort as a sign that you are learning.

- **Problem:** You believe faster output equals better results.
  **Solution:** Speed helps with Zone 1 tasks, but Zone 2 mastery requires deliberate resistance. Slow down intentionally to build long-term intelligence.
