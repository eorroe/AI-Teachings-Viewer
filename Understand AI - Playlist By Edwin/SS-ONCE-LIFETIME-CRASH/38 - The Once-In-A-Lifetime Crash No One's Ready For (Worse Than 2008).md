# The Once-In-A-Lifetime Crash No One's Ready For (Worse Than 2008?)

## Overview

According to the speaker, this AI teaching provides a playbook for professionals and entrepreneurs to assess their AI risk, position themselves in Layer 3 (apps and services) of the AI stack, and execute a 6-step action plan to futureproof their careers against AI-driven job displacement. According to the speaker, the RAIL framework identifies companies or projects with strong demos but no real customer revenue or traction. According to the speaker, real value is captured across the three layers of the AI stack (infrastructure, frontier models, and apps and services). According to the speaker, you can cultivate rigor, relationships, and resilience to thrive whether or not an AI bubble bursts.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When evaluating whether your AI project or company has sustainable revenue and real customer traction
- When assessing your own job security and exposure to AI automation
- When deciding where to focus your career, startup, or consulting efforts in the AI economy
- When you want to automate repetitive work and reinvest time in high-value strategic activities
- When preparing for an AI-driven market shift and want to build durable positions

## Steps

### Step 1: Apply the RAIL Framework to Assess Risk

Take 50 seconds to answer four questions about your AI project, product, or role:

- **R - Revenue:** Is your AI used by real paying customers? If the answer is "we're still piloting" or "eventually," that is a red flag. No revenue means no safety when conditions worsen.
- **A - Acceleration:** Can you deliver something real to customers within 2 weeks? Identify where friction exists and determine how AI removes it. If you cannot show real speed or efficiency gains from AI, you are at risk.
- **I - Inmarket:** Is your AI tool in the hands of real users gathering real data? If it only lives in a test environment, it is a hobby, not a business asset.
- **L - Learning:** Are you learning from real customer use? One week of watching customers break your product teaches you more than 6 months of internal testing.

If you answer "no" to 2 out of 4 questions, your company or project is in the flood zone — meaning it has strong demos but zero customers and zero cash.

### Step 2: Choose the Right Layer in the AI Stack

Ignore noise such as orchestration layers, vector databases, and tensor chips. Focus on the three layers where the speaker says money is made:

- **Layer 1 - Infrastructure:** Chip makers, cloud providers, energy providers. Requires massive capital. Only viable if you are Nvidia or have sovereign fund backing.
- **Layer 2 - Frontier Models:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, XAI, and surrounding tooling. Consolidating rapidly. According to the speaker, price per token has dropped 98% in 18 months. According to the speaker, open-source models now provide 80% of frontier model capability at lower cost. Do not start a frontier model company.
- **Layer 3 - Apps and Services:** The interface and solution layer where value is captured. This is where you should focus if starting something new. It splits into three paths:
  - **Horizontal AI apps:** Serve broad markets (for example, Gamma for presentations, Perplexity for AI search). These products depend on scale for profitability.
  - **Vertical AI apps:** Go deep in a niche (for example, Cursor for developers, OpenEvidence for medicine). Smaller market but you own it.
  - **AI services:** The implementation layer (for example, Scale AI for data work, boutique integration firms charging $50,000 per month to help traditional companies plug AI into workflows).

### Step 3: Audit Your Job Into Scripted vs. Strategic Work

Divide your daily activities into two categories:

- **Scripted work:** Mind-numbing, repetitive tasks such as processing data, summarizing meetings, drafting standard internal emails, and writing boilerplate code. These are the activities AI will automate first.
- **Strategic work:** Solving problems no one has seen before, building client relationships, designing system architecture, and catching edge cases that AI misses.

### Step 4: Fire Yourself From Scripted Work

Automate all scripted tasks before your employer does. This is an efficiency play that secures your position by demonstrating AI leverage rather than waiting to be replaced.

### Step 5: Reinvest Time in Developing Your Human Edge

Shift time from execution to deep strategic thinking. Disconnect from constant scrolling, take long walks, and create space for imagination rather than information consumption. This is where you connect dots others cannot see.

### Step 6: Cultivate the Three Rs

According to the speaker, the top 1% who thrive in the AI economy share three traits—rigor, relationships, and resilience:

- **Rigor:** Develop deep domain mastery, not surface-level knowledge. Evaluate edge cases and understand your data and process intimately. According to the speaker, a BCG and Harvard study found that consultants using GPT-4 with deep domain understanding achieved 40% better results, versus 12% for those without deep domain understanding.
- **Relationships:** Build trust and human connection. In a world where AI can generate outputs, according to the speaker, human trust is what differentiates people who advance in their careers. According to the speaker, while working at a company in New York about a decade ago, keeping an office door open and having late-night conversations with a colleague led to a relationship that, years later, resulted in that colleague becoming the CEO of a $150 million company. According to the speaker, every major career move in their own career—Wall Street, tech, CEO role, board seats—came from relationships, not resumes.
- **Resilience:** Adapt, pivot, and stay in the game after setbacks. External factors such as titles, roles, markets, and technologies rise and fall, but rigor, relationships, and resilience remain constant.

## Examples

### Example 1: Using RAIL to Evaluate an AI Project

You lead an internal AI assistant project. Answer the four RAIL questions:
- Revenue: No paying customers. **No.**
- Acceleration: You can demo a prototype in 2 weeks. **Yes.**
- Inmarket: It is deployed to 3 departments. **Yes.**
- Learning: You gather weekly feedback from users. **Yes.**

Result: 1 "no" out of 4. The project is not in the flood zone.

### Example 2: Using RAIL to Evaluate an AI Product

Your company built an AI customer support tool.
- Revenue: No clear path to monetization. **No.**
- Acceleration: Features take months to ship. **No.**
- Inmarket: Only in pilot with one client. **Yes.**
- Learning: Minimal real-world usage data. **No.**

Result: 3 "no" out of 4. The product is in the flood zone.

### Example 3: Choosing Layer 3 - Vertical AI App

Instead of building a new large language model, build a vertical AI tool for healthcare compliance. Small market, but you own the niche and can charge premium prices because of deep domain expertise.

### Example 4: Automating Scripted Work

A software engineer spends 10 hours per week writing boilerplate code, summarizing meeting notes, and generating standard reports. Using AI coding assistants and automation tools, the engineer automates these tasks and reinvests the 10 hours into system design and client architecture reviews.

### Example 5: Applying the Three Rs - OpenEvidence

According to the speaker, OpenEvidence achieved a $3.5 billion valuation and 40% adoption among US doctors by combining rigor (meticulous peer-reviewed research and human feedback), deep domain understanding, and resilience in a crowded healthcare AI market.

- Audit your job weekly using the four RAIL questions to stay aware of flood zone signals.
- Automate scripted work proactively before your employer forces it.
- Focus career and business efforts on Layer 3 (Apps and Services) in the AI stack.
- Choose a vertical AI niche where you can own deep domain expertise.
- Invest in relationships actively — schedule time to mentor, listen, and connect with colleagues.
- Practice deep thinking through walks, disconnection, and imagination rather than constant information consumption.
- Treat learning from real customer feedback as non-negotiable — internal testing is insufficient.
- Build resilience by treating titles and roles as temporary and focusing on enduring skills and networks.

- Do not maintain the status quo — it is the fastest path to the flood zone.
- Do not rely on surface-level AI tool knowledge without deep domain mastery.
- Do not skip the strategic work because scripted automation feels productive.
