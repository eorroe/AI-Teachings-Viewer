# The World Needs AI, But There's a Problem

## Overview

This AI teaching outlines five critical problems with AI that every professional and individual who uses AI tools must understand to navigate responsibly: the blackbox problem, AI bias, cognitive atrophy from over-reliance, the erosion of truth through deepfakes, and the unstoppable AI arms race. It provides actionable guidance on how to recognize these risks and adopt practices that protect yourself and society.

## When to Follow These AI Teachings

- When you use AI tools in your daily work or personal life
- When you rely on AI for hiring, legal, or consequential decisions
- When you share or consume AI-generated content online
- When you want to maintain your cognitive skills while using AI productively
- When you care about the effects of AI on society and want to advocate for responsible AI development

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Blackbox Problem

AI developers build models from billions of parameters across hundreds of layers that even the models' own creators cannot fully explain or debug. Treat AI outputs as probabilistic suggestions rather than fully understood decisions. Support and follow the field of explainable AI (interpretability), which aims to make AI behavior transparent. Advocate for mandatory safety standards and regulations for AI models, similar to those required for food, medicine, and cars.

### Step 2: Audit AI for Bias Before Acting on Its Output

AI is trained on human data that contains historical prejudice and stereotyping embedded over decades. Before using AI for hiring, lending, legal, or any high-stakes decision, run a bias audit: test the AI with inputs representing diverse demographic groups, including inputs for protected characteristics such as race, gender, age, and disability status, and adversarial edge cases, then check whether it produces discriminatory outcomes. Make bias checks non-negotiable in your workflow. If you would not accept discrimination from a human recruiter or judge, do not accept it from AI. Recognize the bias amplification loop: humans have biases, data feeds AI, AI amplifies biases, humans absorb biased results, and the cycle repeats. Break the loop by consciously checking for bias at every step.

### Step 3: Use AI as a Tool, Not a Crutch

AI can displace cognitive exercise. Research from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) shows that people who use AI every day develop weaker neural connectivity and perform worse on cognitive tests after four months. Treat AI like a sparring partner: use it to refine your thinking, not replace it. Focus on developing skills that AI cannot replicate: servant leadership, creativity, emotional intelligence, and critical judgment. Prioritize hard thinking work yourself to preserve cognitive strength. This is especially critical for people aged 20 to 30, who are entering the workforce at a time when entry-level role unemployment for that age group spiked above the prior baseline, according to the speaker, and who need to exercise their brains to grow professionally.

### Step 4: Build Media Hygiene to Protect Truth

AI-generated deepfakes were weaponized across elections in the US, Taiwan, Indonesia, and South Africa in 2024, according to the speaker. The liar's dividend means that once people know media can be faked, they stop believing all evidence, even real evidence. Question everything you see, verify sources before sharing, and do not blindly trust content just because it looks authentic. Fact-check everything, including content generated or flagged by AI. Invest in building human trust by being the person who verifies sources, asks real questions, and shares wisely. Support emerging solutions like cryptographic watermarks, AI detection models, and standards like C2PA (Coalition for Content Authenticity and Provenance) that verify the origin of images and videos.

### Step 5: Participate in the AI Governance Conversation

According to the speaker, the most powerful AI on the planet is controlled by four American companies (Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, and XAI), and the AI arms race is escalating. In 2024, US private AI investment was higher than China's investment in the same period. The race creates a paradox where no company can afford to pause for safety because competitors will not pause. You have a choice: join the global conversation about building safe and responsible AI, support companies and leaders who champion safety over speed, and contact your elected representatives to ask for AI regulations. According to the speaker, the most powerful AI on the planet is controlled by four American companies (Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, and XAI) run by CEOs the public did not elect, and public pressure and policy are the only checks on their power.

## Examples

### Example 1: Amazon's Biased Hiring Tool

Amazon built an AI hiring tool trained on 10 years of its own resumes. The AI learned that male candidates were historically favored, so it downgraded resumes that mentioned women's chess clubs or women's colleges. The result was a system that systematically penalized female candidates. This demonstrates how historical human bias becomes encoded in AI and then amplified. The fix is to audit training data and AI outputs for discriminatory patterns before deploying any AI for hiring decisions.

### Example 2: ProPublica's Investigation of COMPAS

ProPublica found that the COMPAS algorithm, used in US courts to predict recidivism, was almost twice as likely to label Black defendants as high-risk compared to white defendants with similar criminal records. This shows how AI bias in high-stakes domains like criminal justice can cause real harm. The lesson is to never allow AI to make autonomous decisions in domains where human lives and freedoms are at stake without rigorous bias auditing and human oversight.

### Example 3: ChatGPT's Unpredictable Behavior in February 2024

In February 2024, ChatGPT began generating nonsensical output such as "the broker of the celestial of the meat" for millions of users. OpenAI's own creators could not explain the cause for hours. This incident illustrates the blackbox problem: even the developers of the world's most advanced AI cannot fully understand or predict its behavior. The takeaway is to always have human review and fallback processes for critical AI-dependent workflows.

### Example 4: The Liar's Dividend and Deepfake Elections

In 2024, AI deepfakes were weaponized across elections in the US, Taiwan, Indonesia, and South Africa, according to the speaker. Once audiences learned that video and audio could be faked, they began dismissing real evidence as fake as well. A corrupt politician caught on a real damning tape could simply claim it was AI-generated, and audiences would shrug. The lesson is to build personal and institutional media verification habits before the problem becomes unmanageable.

## Keep In Mind

- The blackbox problem means AI models cannot be fully debugged or understood, even by their creators.
- AI bias is not a future problem; it is already embedded in systems used for hiring, lending, criminal justice, and healthcare. The bias amplification loop means that using biased AI makes your own biases stronger over time.
- Cognitive atrophy from AI over-reliance is measurable: MIT research shows weaker neural connectivity and worse cognitive test performance after four months of daily AI use.
- The liar's dividend means that widespread deepfakes will erode trust in all evidence, real or fake. Once truth is destabilized, it is difficult to restore — the speaker notes that fakes get better faster than detection tools, making truth harder to prove and debunking slower and more expensive.
- The AI arms race is driven by competitive pressure: every company knows it cannot pause because competitors will not pause. According to the speaker, individual and regulatory action is the only way to introduce safety considerations into the race.
- AI models face fewer mandatory safety standards than food, medicine, and cars. The gap between AI capability and AI governance is a dangerous asymmetry of our time.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never rely on AI output for safety-critical decisions without independent human verification. AI can produce plausible but incorrect or harmful outputs.
- AI-generated deepfakes were weaponized across elections in the US, Taiwan, Indonesia, and South Africa in 2024, according to the speaker. Always verify the provenance of media before acting on it or sharing it.
- Bias in AI systems can cause discrimination in hiring, lending, criminal justice, and healthcare. Audit AI tools before deployment and maintain human oversight for all consequential decisions.
- Over-reliance on AI for cognitive work can degrade your own strategic thinking and problem-solving abilities. Maintain a balance between AI assistance and independent thinking.
- The concentration of AI power in four unelected companies creates systemic risk. Diversify your AI tooling where possible and support open-source alternatives.
- Deepfake technology is advancing faster than detection tools. Assume that any unverified media could be AI-generated and act accordingly.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Assuming AI outputs are correct because they sound confident and authoritative
  **Solution:** Always independently verify AI outputs, especially for high-stakes decisions. Confidence does not equal correctness.

- **Problem:** Using AI for consequential decisions without bias testing
  **Solution:** Run bias audits with diverse test inputs before deploying any AI for consequential decisions. Make bias checks as routine as spellcheck.

- **Problem:** Using AI as a complete replacement for thinking and writing
  **Solution:** Use AI to refine and spar with your own thinking, not to generate it for you. Keep exercising your cognitive muscles independently.

- **Problem:** Sharing AI-generated media without verifying its authenticity
  **Solution:** Question, verify, and fact-check before sharing. Do not become an unwitting vector for misinformation.

- **Problem:** Assuming someone else will solve the AI safety and governance problem
  **Solution:** Join the conversation yourself. Contact representatives, support safety-focused companies, and advocate for regulation. The race will not slow down without public pressure.

- **Problem:** Believing that open-source AI from any single country will democratize the technology
  **Solution:** Recognize that the AI market is dominated by four well-funded American companies with massive infrastructure advantages. Open-source models are promising but currently make a small dent in the market.
